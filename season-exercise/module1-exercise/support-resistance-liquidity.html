01. Support & Resistance Are Areas

Support and resistance are better understood as areas or zones where price has reacted, rather than perfect mathematical lines. The notes emphasize important zones, reactions and rejection, along with the idea that price may move slightly beyond an area before reacting.

Support ZoneAn area where buying interest or previous reactions may make it harder for price to continue lower.
Resistance ZoneAn area where selling interest or previous reactions may make it harder for price to continue higher.
Zone, Not Exact LinePrice can enter, leave or briefly move through an area without invalidating the concept automatically.
SUPPORT AND RESISTANCE AS ZONES

02. Different Types of Areas Traders Watch

Traders can mark important areas in different ways. The most useful lesson is not to memorize a huge list of lines, but to understand why a price area matters and how price reacts when it reaches that area.

Horizontal ZonesPrevious highs and lows or repeated reaction areas can create important horizontal zones.
TrendlinesA line connecting meaningful swing points can help visualize a directional path.
Previous Reaction AreasAreas where price previously showed rejection, breakout or repeated interaction can remain important.
Breakout / Retest AreasA previous boundary that price breaks may later become an area to watch for a retest and reaction.
Range BoundariesThe upper and lower boundaries of a range can behave as repeated areas of interest.
ConfluenceSeveral pieces of structural evidence can overlap around the same area, increasing its importance as context.
TRENDLINE + HORIZONTAL AREA — CONCEPTUAL

03. Rejection, Breakout and Retest

A price area becomes more useful when you study the reaction around it. Price can reject an area, break through it, or break and later return to test the area again.

RejectionPrice reaches an area and moves away from it instead of continuing through.
BreakoutPrice moves through an important boundary or zone.
RetestAfter a breakout, price may return toward the broken area and react again.
BREAKOUT → RETEST → POSSIBLE REACTION

04. What Is Liquidity?

Liquidity describes the availability of orders that can help transactions take place. In chart-based Price Action discussions, traders often watch areas where many market participants may place orders, such as around obvious highs, lows and ranges.

Equal HighsRepeated or nearly equal highs can become an obvious area watched by market participants.
Equal LowsRepeated or nearly equal lows can likewise become an obvious area of interest.
Range EdgesHighs and lows around a visible range can become areas where orders accumulate.
OBVIOUS HIGH / LOW AREAS — LIQUIDITY CONCEPT
Important:

"Liquidity" in chart analysis is a context concept. Do not assume that every obvious high or low will definitely be swept, and do not treat a liquidity area as an automatic entry signal.

05. Liquidity Sweep — Basic Concept

A liquidity sweep describes a situation where price moves into or through an obvious high/low area, interacts with the orders around that area, and then may reverse or continue. The important learning point is to observe what happens after the interaction.

LIQUIDITY AREA → SWEEP → OBSERVE REACTION

09. Quick Revision

  • Support and resistance should generally be treated as zones or areas rather than perfect lines.
  • Important areas can come from horizontal reactions, trendlines, range boundaries and previous reaction zones.
  • Price can reject an area, break through it or return to retest a broken area.
  • Liquidity is a context concept describing areas where orders may be concentrated or available for transactions.
  • Equal highs, equal lows and visible range edges can become obvious liquidity areas.
  • A liquidity sweep can involve price moving into or through an obvious high/low area.
  • The reaction and broader market context matter after a sweep.
  • No single zone, breakout, retest or sweep guarantees a trade outcome.
