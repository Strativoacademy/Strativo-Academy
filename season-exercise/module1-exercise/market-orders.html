01. What Is a Market Order?

A market order is an instruction to execute a buy or sell at the best available price in the market at that moment, subject to available liquidity and the execution conditions of the broker or venue.

The key idea is that the trader prioritizes getting the order executed rather than specifying one exact execution price. Because the market is moving and liquidity changes, the final execution price can differ from the price visible when the order was sent.

BUY NOW
EXECUTES AGAINST AVAILABLE ASK LIQUIDITY

MARKET SELL
SELL NOW
EXECUTES AGAINST AVAILABLE BID LIQUIDITY
IMPORTANT

A market order does not mean "guaranteed exact price." It means the order is sent for execution under the current market conditions.

02. Market Buy vs Market Sell

Market Buy A buy order is normally executed at the available ask side of the quote, subject to liquidity and execution conditions.
Market Sell A sell order is normally executed at the available bid side of the quote, subject to liquidity and execution conditions.
Price Is Not Static The displayed bid and ask can change between the moment an order is sent and the moment it is executed.
BID / ASK — BASIC EXECUTION MODEL

03. Why Execution Price Can Differ

The market can change while an order is travelling for execution. Liquidity can also become thinner or more fragmented. This means the requested action may be executed at a different price from the quote the trader saw when pressing Buy or Sell.

Spread The bid and ask are different prices. That difference is a transaction cost built into the quoted market.
Slippage Execution can occur at a different price from the price expected or displayed when the order was submitted.
Liquidity Available liquidity affects how easily an order can be matched without moving through multiple available price levels.
WHY A MARKET ORDER MAY MOVE THROUGH AVAILABLE PRICES

04. What Affects Market-Order Execution?

Factor What It Means Why It Matters
Bid / Ask The two sides of the quoted market. Buy and sell orders interact with different sides.
Spread Difference between bid and ask. Creates an immediate pricing difference between the two sides.
Liquidity Available executable interest around current prices. Thin liquidity can make execution less predictable.
Volatility Speed and size of price changes. Fast markets can change the quote rapidly.
Slippage Difference between expected and actual execution price. The executed price may not equal the displayed/requested price.

08. Quick Revision

  • A market order prioritizes execution under current market conditions.
  • A market buy normally interacts with available ask liquidity.
  • A market sell normally interacts with available bid liquidity.
  • The spread is the difference between bid and ask.
  • Liquidity affects how easily an order can be executed around current prices.
  • Fast price movement can cause the final execution price to differ from the displayed price.
  • That execution-price difference is commonly described as slippage.
