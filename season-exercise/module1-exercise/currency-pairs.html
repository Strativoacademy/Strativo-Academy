01. Read the Pair Correctly

A currency pair represents the value of one currency relative to another. Forex quotes contain two currencies because the market expresses a relationship between them. The first currency is the base currency and the second is the quote currency.

FIRST CURRENCY
EUR
BASE CURRENCY
/
SECOND CURRENCY
USD
QUOTE CURRENCY
EUR / USD = 1.1000

One unit of EUR is valued at 1.1000 units of USD in this quotation.

Base Currency

The base currency is written first. It is the currency whose value is being expressed relative to the second currency.

Quote Currency

The quote currency is written second. It tells you how many units correspond to one unit of the base currency.

HOW TO READ A FOREX QUOTE

02. What Does a Rising or Falling Pair Mean?

When the quoted price of a pair rises, the base currency is becoming more valuable relative to the quote currency. When the quoted price falls, the base currency is becoming less valuable relative to the quote currency.

EUR/USD Rises

More USD are represented by one EUR, so the base currency is stronger relative to the quote within the pair.

EUR/USD Falls

Fewer USD are represented by one EUR, so the base currency is weaker relative to the quote within the pair.

PAIR MOVEMENT — RELATIVE VALUE

03. Pair Categories

Currency pairs are commonly grouped into major pairs, minor or cross pairs, and exotic pairs. These categories describe how the currencies relate to the U.S. dollar and common market usage.

CategoryExampleBasic Idea
MajorEUR/USDCommonly traded USD pair involving a major global currency.
MajorUSD/JPYUSD paired with the Japanese yen.
Cross / MinorEUR/GBPTwo major currencies without USD in the pair.
ExoticUSD/INRCommonly describes a major currency paired with an emerging-market currency.
  • Major examples: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF, AUD/USD, USD/CAD, NZD/USD.
  • Cross example: EUR/GBP has no USD in the pair.
  • Exotic example: USD/INR pairs a major currency with an emerging-market currency.
