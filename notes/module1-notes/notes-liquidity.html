Liquidity

Liquidity refers to the areas on a chart where a large volume of resting buy and sell orders are clustered, often above previous highs and below previous lows. Institutional algorithms target these liquidity pools to fill large positions, resulting in sweeps and rapid price reversals.

Master liquidity from foundation to advanced price-action concepts: pools, BSL/SSL, equal highs/lows, internal/external liquidity, session and range liquidity, sweeps, inducement, displacement, imbalance, structure shifts, premium/discount and practical liquidity mapping.

💧 Liquidity 🟢 Buy-Side 🔴 Sell-Side 🎯 Liquidity Sweep

01. What Is Liquidity?

LIQUIDITY IS A POSSIBILITY, NOT A GUARANTEE

On a normal chart we cannot see every participant or every hidden order. Liquidity zones are therefore analytical areas where trading interest may be concentrated.

WHY OBVIOUS LEVELS MATTER

Highs, lows, equal highs and equal lows are easy for many traders to identify. Different traders can use the same reference for stops, breakout entries, exits or targets.

PROFESSIONAL MINDSET

Use liquidity to ask better questions about price behaviour. Do not assume that a particular institution must intentionally hunt a specific trader's stop.

In simple terms, liquidity refers to the availability of orders that can participate in buying and selling. In trading education, the word is also commonly used to describe areas on a chart where many orders may be concentrated.

These areas can develop around obvious highs, lows, equal highs, equal lows, previous swing points and other locations that many market participants can see.

SIMPLE DEFINITION

Think of chart liquidity as an area where a relatively large amount of trading interest or orders may be located.

⚠️ IMPORTANT

We cannot directly see every market participant's orders from a normal price chart. Liquidity zones are therefore areas of analysis and inference, not guaranteed maps of hidden orders.

02. Why Does Liquidity Matter?

Understanding liquidity can help explain why price sometimes moves toward obvious highs or lows before making a larger directional move.

01

Obvious Levels

Traders often watch clear highs, lows and equal-price areas.

02

Order Concentration

Stop orders and other trading interest may become concentrated around obvious locations.

03

Price Movement

Price can move through an obvious area and then continue or reverse depending on market conditions.

03. Buy-Side Liquidity

ABOVE HIGHS

Above a high there may be short protective stops and breakout buy orders. When triggered, these orders can create buying activity.

BSL IS NOT ONLY STOP-LOSSES

The area can contain different order types. 'Buy-side liquidity' describes the potential direction of orders that become active, not one specific trader group.

MARK THE AREA

Use major highs, equal highs and obvious range highs as references. The exact location and significance depend on timeframe and context.

Buy-side liquidity is commonly discussed as liquidity located above important highs. One reason is that traders who are short may place protective stop orders above swing highs or other obvious resistance areas.

🟢 Above Highs

Obvious highs can become areas that attract attention from traders looking for breakout opportunities and from traders protecting short positions.

📈 Buy Orders

When price trades upward through an area, stop orders from short positions can become buy orders when triggered.

BUY-SIDE LIQUIDITY ABOVE A HIGH CONCEPTUAL CHART

04. Sell-Side Liquidity

BELOW LOWS

Below a low there may be long protective stops and sell-stop breakout orders. Triggered orders can create selling activity.

SSL IS NOT ONLY STOP-LOSSES

Different traders can have different reasons for placing orders around the same low. Treat the area as a possible concentration, not a visible hidden pool.

MARK THE AREA

Use major lows, equal lows and obvious range lows. Always compare them with the larger market structure.

Sell-side liquidity is commonly discussed as liquidity located below important lows. Traders holding long positions may place protective stop orders below swing lows or other obvious support areas.

🔴 Below Lows

Obvious lows can become areas watched by traders because protective stops and breakout orders may be positioned around them.

📉 Sell Orders

When price trades downward through an area, stop orders from long positions can become sell orders when triggered.

SELL-SIDE LIQUIDITY BELOW A LOW CONCEPTUAL CHART

05. Equal Highs and Equal Lows

Equal highs and equal lows are visually obvious areas where price has reacted around approximately the same level more than once. Because many traders can identify these areas, they can become important liquidity reference points.

Equal Highs

Repeated highs around a similar price area can create a visible pool of potential buy-side liquidity above them.

Equal Lows

Repeated lows around a similar price area can create a visible pool of potential sell-side liquidity below them.

EQUAL HIGHS + EQUAL LOWS LIQUIDITY MAP

06. Liquidity Above Highs

Imagine a clear swing high on the chart. Traders who are short may place protective stops above that high. Other traders may also be watching the same high for a breakout.

Because the area is obvious, the region above the high can become important when analysing possible buy-side liquidity.

🧠 EASY MEMORY

High → look above → possible buy-side liquidity.

07. Liquidity Below Lows

The same concept works below lows. Traders who are long may place protective stops below an obvious swing low. Other traders may watch that low for a downside breakout.

Therefore, the area below a clear low can become important when analysing possible sell-side liquidity.

🧠 EASY MEMORY

Low → look below → possible sell-side liquidity.

08. What Is a Liquidity Sweep?

THE COMPLETE SEQUENCE

A useful sequence is: obvious liquidity reference → price trades through it → price behaviour after the level is reached → possible structural consequence.

SWEEP DOES NOT EQUAL REVERSAL

A sweep can be followed by rejection, continuation or consolidation. The post-sweep candles are essential.

BETTER CONFIRMATION

Depending on your rules, look for displacement, a close back inside a range, a break of relevant internal structure or another predefined condition.

A liquidity sweep is a term traders commonly use when price moves through an obvious high or low and then reacts. The move through the area can trigger orders positioned around that level.

A sweep does not automatically mean price must reverse. After moving through liquidity, price can reverse, continue in the same direction, or consolidate.

SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Price moves above an obvious high, trades beyond it, and then quickly returns below the high. Traders may describe this as a buy-side liquidity sweep.

BUY-SIDE LIQUIDITY SWEEP HIGH → SWEEP → RETURN

09. Sell-Side Liquidity Sweep

A sell-side liquidity sweep is commonly described when price moves below an obvious low, trades through that area and then reacts back upward.

SELL-SIDE LIQUIDITY SWEEP LOW → SWEEP → RETURN

10. Liquidity and Stop-Loss Orders

Stop-loss orders are one reason obvious highs and lows can become important when studying liquidity. For example, a trader holding a long position may place a protective stop below a relevant low.

If many traders make similar decisions around the same obvious area, the area can become important in liquidity analysis.

🟢

Long Position

Protective stop may be placed below a relevant low.

🔴

Short Position

Protective stop may be placed above a relevant high.

🎯

Obvious Level

A clearly visible high or low can attract attention from many traders.

⚠️ IMPORTANT

Never assume that every stop-loss order is located at exactly the same price. The chart only gives us clues about where orders may be concentrated.

11. Liquidity + Support & Resistance

Support and resistance describe important price areas. Liquidity analysis asks another question: where might orders be concentrated around those areas?

Resistance + Buy-Side Liquidity

An obvious resistance or swing-high area may have potential buy-side liquidity above it.

Support + Sell-Side Liquidity

An obvious support or swing-low area may have potential sell-side liquidity below it.

🧠 CONNECT THE CONCEPTS

Support → look below for possible sell-side liquidity. Resistance → look above for possible buy-side liquidity.

12. Liquidity Sweep vs Normal Breakout

ACCEPTANCE

If price crosses a level and continues to trade and close on the new side, the market is showing evidence of acceptance.

REJECTION

If price crosses and quickly returns, the move is showing evidence of rejection or failure to hold the new territory.

RETEST

A breakout followed by a return to the broken level can provide additional information. Never assume every retest must hold.

A move beyond a high or low does not automatically mean that a liquidity sweep occurred. The subsequent price behavior matters.

Possible Sweep

Price moves through an obvious level and then quickly returns or reacts back toward the previous range.

Possible Breakout

Price moves beyond the level and maintains acceptance above or below it instead of immediately returning.

⚠️ DO NOT LABEL TOO QUICKLY

A candle temporarily trading beyond a level is not enough by itself to determine whether the move is a sweep or a genuine breakout.

13. Step-by-Step Liquidity Analysis

01

Find Obvious Highs and Lows

Start with visible swing points that stand out on the chart.

02

Look for Equal Highs or Equal Lows

Repeated highs or lows around a similar price can become important reference areas.

03

Mark the Liquidity Area

Identify the area above highs or below lows where liquidity may potentially be concentrated.

04

Wait for Price to Reach It

Do not assume price must visit the area. It is only a potential area of interest.

05

Observe the Reaction

Watch whether price rejects the area, breaks through and continues, or returns inside the previous range.

06

Combine With Structure

Liquidity should be studied alongside market structure, support and resistance and broader price behavior.

14. Common Liquidity Locations

  • Above obvious swing highs.
  • Below obvious swing lows.
  • Above equal highs.
  • Below equal lows.
  • Around highly visible previous highs and lows.
  • Around major support and resistance areas.
  • Around obvious range boundaries.
KEY IDEA

The more obvious an area is to many market participants, the more useful it may be to investigate as a potential liquidity reference.

15. Liquidity Inside a Trading Range

Liquidity is not limited to large trends. It can also appear around the boundaries of a range.

16. Liquidity Does Not Mean "Smart Money Must Take Stops"

Liquidity concepts are useful for analysing price behavior, but they should not be treated as proof that a specific institution or "smart money" must intentionally hunt a trader's stop.

Markets contain many participants with different objectives, strategies, time horizons and order types. Price movement around obvious levels can have multiple causes.

PROFESSIONAL THINKING

Use liquidity as a framework for analysing possible order concentration and price behavior, not as a guaranteed explanation for every market move.

17. Common Mistakes

ANOTHER MAJOR MISTAKE — OVERLABELING

If every wick is called a sweep, the word loses meaning. A sweep model should have clear rules.

ANOTHER MISTAKE — PREDICTING THE TARGET

Finding liquidity does not mean price must visit it next. Price can reverse before the level or continue beyond it.

ANOTHER MISTAKE — IGNORING CONTEXT

Liquidity should be read with timeframe, structure, volatility and the surrounding price map.

  • Assuming every high contains huge liquidity. A high is only a potential reference point.
  • Assuming every wick is a liquidity sweep. A wick can have many explanations.
  • Buying immediately after a sell-side sweep. A sweep alone does not guarantee reversal.
  • Selling immediately after a buy-side sweep. Price can continue upward after taking an obvious high.
  • Ignoring the higher timeframe. Context matters.
  • Ignoring market structure. Liquidity should not be analysed in isolation.
  • Calling every breakout a fakeout. Some breakouts genuinely continue.
  • Thinking liquidity predicts the future. It provides a framework for analysing possible behavior, not certainty.

🎯 Final Summary

Liquidity is an important concept for understanding where trading interest may become concentrated. Obvious highs, lows, equal highs, equal lows and major price areas can become useful reference points.

Buy-side liquidity is commonly discussed above highs, while sell-side liquidity is commonly discussed below lows. Price may move through these areas and trigger orders before continuing, reversing or consolidating.

A liquidity sweep describes a move through an obvious level followed by a reaction, but a sweep is not a guaranteed reversal signal. Always examine what price does after the level is reached.

THE CORE LESSON

Find the obvious level → identify the possible liquidity area → wait for price → observe the reaction → combine it with structure and context.

21. Internal vs External Liquidity

EXTERNAL LIQUIDITY

Major highs/lows outside a defined dealing range are commonly treated as external liquidity references.

INTERNAL LIQUIDITY

Smaller highs/lows inside the range are internal liquidity. They may be taken before a larger external objective.

HOW TO USE IT

Build the large map first, then add only relevant internal swings.

INTERNAL VS EXTERNAL LIQUIDITY

22. Liquidity Pools

COMMON POOLS

Equal highs, equal lows, swing highs/lows, range boundaries, session highs/lows and previous day/week levels are common liquidity references.

POOL QUALITY

Visibility, timeframe, repetition and structural importance all matter.

DO NOT OVERLOAD THE CHART

A clean map with a few important pools is more useful than dozens of minor levels.

23. Swing Liquidity

MAJOR SWINGS

Major swing highs and lows are broad structural references.

INTERNAL SWINGS

Smaller swing points can become internal liquidity inside a trend or range.

PRACTICAL RULE

Mark the major extremes first. Add internal levels only when they explain current price behaviour.

26. Session Liquidity

SESSION HIGHS/LOWS

Active sessions can create identifiable intraday extremes that later become reference points.

NO GUARANTEE

A session high does not have to be swept. News and changing volatility can completely alter behaviour.

WORKFLOW

Mark the prior session high and low, then observe the next active session as price approaches them.

SESSION HIGH / LOW MAP

27. Trendline and Diagonal Liquidity

DIAGONAL REFERENCES

Trendlines can become visible reference areas when several swings align.

LOWER PRECISION

A diagonal depends on selected anchor points and changes with time. Horizontal highs/lows are more objective.

BEST PRACTICE

Use swing points as primary evidence and diagonal structure as supporting context.

28. Inducement and Bait Liquidity

COMMON EDUCATIONAL DEFINITION

Inducement is often used for an internal feature that may attract participation before a larger liquidity objective.

EXAMPLE

An internal swing forms beneath a major high. Traders may focus on the internal structure before price reaches the larger high.

CAUTION

Do not assume every internal swing was deliberately engineered to trap traders. Treat inducement as a model with explicit rules.

INTERNAL FEATURE → EXTERNAL LIQUIDITY

29. Liquidity Raid, Grab and Sweep Terminology

TERMS VARY

Different educators use raid, grab, stop hunt and sweep differently.

STRATIVO RULE

Teach the observable behaviour first: price interacts with an obvious liquidity reference and the following reaction is studied.

WHY THIS HELPS

Students learn chart behaviour instead of memorising labels.

30. Liquidity Void, Imbalance and Fair Value Gap

LIQUIDITY VOID

A liquidity void is commonly used for an area where price moved rapidly with relatively little visible two-way interaction.

IMBALANCE / FVG

An imbalance or fair value gap is commonly defined using a three-candle relationship. Exact definitions vary between strategies.

CONNECTION

A sweep can be followed by displacement, and displacement can leave an imbalance. These are separate observations that can be combined.

SWEEP → DISPLACEMENT → IMBALANCE

31. Displacement After a Sweep

DISPLACEMENT

Displacement describes decisive directional price movement.

WHY IT MATTERS

A sweep followed by strong movement away gives more information than a wick alone.

STRUCTURAL CONSEQUENCE

Study whether the displacement also breaks a relevant internal swing.

SWEEP + DISPLACEMENT

32. Liquidity + BOS / CHOCH / MSS

BOS

Break of Structure commonly describes a break of a relevant structural swing. The exact rule must be defined.

CHOCH / MSS

These terms are commonly used for potential changes in directional behaviour. Definitions vary by educator.

SEQUENCE

Liquidity reference → sweep → displacement → structural break is a more developed narrative than 'wick = reversal'.

LIQUIDITY → DISPLACEMENT → STRUCTURE

33. Premium, Equilibrium and Discount

DEALING RANGE

Premium and discount are commonly taught by dividing a meaningful range around its midpoint.

LIQUIDITY CONNECTION

Range highs/lows can be external liquidity references. Premium/discount adds positional context.

DO NOT USE RANDOM RANGES

The range must come from meaningful structure; otherwise the midpoint has little analytical value.

34. Liquidity + Order Blocks

ORDER BLOCK TERM

Order block is a trading-education term with multiple definitions, often referring to a candle or small region associated with a strong subsequent move.

LIQUIDITY CONNECTION

Some frameworks study sweep → displacement → return toward an origin area and call that area an order block.

NO HIDDEN-ORDER CLAIM

A chart region labelled an order block is not direct proof of a bank order.

35. Mitigation and Retest

RETEST

After a breakout or displacement, price may return toward the crossed level.

ACCEPTANCE

Observe whether price holds the new side or falls back through it.

FAILED RETEST

If the level fails again, the continuation thesis needs to be reconsidered.

36. Nested Liquidity Across Timeframes

MULTIPLE LAYERS

A four-hour high can contain many one-minute highs. Small levels can be internal while the four-hour extreme is external.

TOP-DOWN

Map higher-timeframe boundaries first, then lower-timeframe internal liquidity.

CONFLICT

A lower-timeframe reaction does not automatically invalidate higher-timeframe structure.

37. Liquidity Around News and Volatility

WHY NEWS MATTERS

Major economic releases can rapidly change volatility and order flow.

DO NOT LABEL EVERY SPIKE A SWEEP

A news wick can be caused by rapid repricing rather than a clean technical sweep model.

STUDY RULE

Mark major news separately when reviewing historical charts.

39. Educational Liquidity Models

MODEL A — SWEEP AND REACTION

Liquidity reference → price reaches it → sweep → reaction → structure/risk evaluation.

MODEL B — BREAKOUT AND ACCEPTANCE

Level → break → acceptance beyond → possible retest → reassessment.

MODEL C — SWEEP → DISPLACEMENT → STRUCTURE

Liquidity reference → sweep → displacement → relevant structural break → pullback study if required.

RISK RULE

No liquidity model guarantees an outcome. Define invalidation before entering and keep position sizing consistent.

BUY-SIDE EXAMPLE

Price reaches an obvious high → trades above → returns below → bearish displacement → relevant internal low breaks. This is a sequence to study, not an automatic sell signal.

SELL-SIDE EXAMPLE

Price reaches an obvious low → trades below → recovers above → bullish displacement → relevant internal high breaks. Confirmation and risk rules remain necessary.

40. Liquidity Failure Scenarios + Study Lab

SWEEP THEN CONTINUATION

Price can take a high and continue higher. The sweep did not produce the reversal condition.

FALSE STRUCTURAL SHIFT

A lower-timeframe break can occur while higher-timeframe structure remains unchanged.

TOO MANY LEVELS

If everything is liquidity, nothing is important. Keep the map clean.

HINDSIGHT BIAS

Mark charts before revealing future candles.

PRACTICAL JOURNAL

Record level, timeframe, liquidity side, event, reaction, structural consequence and lesson.
