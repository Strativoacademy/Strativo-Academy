01What Is Bid and Ask?

Bid and ask are the two sides of a tradable quote. The bid is the price associated with selling to the market in the basic quote model, while the ask is the price associated with buying from the market. A quote therefore represents two-sided pricing rather than one universal execution price.

Simple Example:
The two numbers are not an unnecessary complication. They explain why immediate buying and selling normally occur at slightly different prices.

Why This Matters

Execution is part of trading. A chart can provide a market idea, but the actual position is opened and closed through quoted prices. Understanding bid, ask and spread helps separate theoretical price movement from practical execution conditions. A trader who understands this is less likely to assume that every displayed price is simultaneously available for buying and selling.

How to Think About It

Think of bid and ask as two sides of the same market. The spread is the distance between those sides. An immediate order interacts with the relevant side of the quote. If the market changes before execution, the available quote can also change. Therefore a price seen a moment ago is not necessarily a guaranteed future fill.

Practical Application

Before trading, identify the instrument, read the current quote, observe the spread, consider the target and stop, calculate position size, and review other costs such as commission and possible slippage. The importance of each variable depends on the strategy, holding period and market conditions.

  • I know which price is bid and which is ask.
  • I know the current spread.
  • I understand which quote side my order uses.
  • I know my entry, stop and target.
  • I checked spread relative to the target.
  • I considered position size and pip value.

  • The trade still fits my overall risk plan.

48Final Summary

Bid and ask are the two sides of a tradable quote. The spread is the distance between them. In the basic market-order model, buys interact with ask and sells interact with bid. Closing a long and closing a short therefore involve the opposite quote sides.

Spread is one part of trading cost. Commission may be separate. Slippage concerns execution differences. Liquidity and volatility can influence the conditions under which quotes and execution behave.

The central lesson is that a chart setup and an execution setup are not exactly the same. The practical workflow is: read the quote → understand bid/ask → observe spread → compare it with expected movement → check costs → check risk and position size → decide.

Remember: A tight spread is useful, but it is not a buy signal. A wide spread is a cost condition, not a sell signal.
