Risk Management

Risk management is the defensive strategy of protecting your trading capital from significant losses. It involves defining a strict risk percentage per trade, calculating precise position sizes, and using stop-loss orders to ensure long-term survivability.

A complete reference for controlling trading exposure. Learn risk per trade, position sizing, stop-loss planning, drawdown limits, total exposure, correlation, news risk, execution risk, journaling and practical pre-trade controls.

01What Is Risk Management?

Risk management is the process of deciding how much capital can be exposed, where a trade becomes invalid, and what limits apply before, during and after trading.

Simple Example:
A trader can have a good setup and still lose. Risk management controls the size of that loss.

Why This Matters

Risk management works best when the decision is made before the trade. The purpose is to prevent one position, one session or one emotional decision from causing disproportionate damage to the account.

How to Apply It

Write the rule, calculate the exposure, verify the conditions and follow the same process consistently. If a condition is outside the plan, waiting is a legitimate decision.

What Is Risk Management?
Important: Risk management controls exposure; it does not guarantee a profitable trade.

The technical setup is valid, but the position is too large. The trade moves against the trader and the loss becomes much larger than the plan allows. The risk problem came from sizing, not from the existence of the setup.

Scenario B — Several Correlated Trades

A trader opens multiple pairs that all expose the account to the same currency factor. Each trade looks acceptable separately, but the combined exposure is excessive.

Scenario C — Daily Limit Reached

After several losses, the account reaches the predefined daily limit. The risk-managed action is to stop trading and review later instead of attempting to recover immediately.

53Common Risk-Management Mistakes

  • Choosing position size before calculating risk.
  • Using maximum available leverage as a target.
  • Moving stops farther away because of emotion.
  • Removing a planned protective exit.
  • Ignoring total exposure across correlated pairs.
  • Increasing size after a loss to recover quickly.
  • Ignoring spread, commission, swap or slippage.
  • Trading after reaching a daily loss limit.
  • Changing risk rules during a losing streak.
  • Testing entries but not testing risk rules.

54Complete Risk Checklist

  • The setup meets my strategy rules.
  • The invalidation point is clearly defined.
  • Maximum monetary or percentage risk is known.
  • Position size is calculated from that risk.
  • Total open exposure is acceptable.
  • Correlated positions have been considered.
  • Spread and costs are acceptable.
  • Important news conditions have been checked.
  • Daily and weekly limits have not been exceeded.
  • I am not trading emotionally.
  • I will not move the stop simply to avoid a loss.
  • I will record the result after the trade.

55Final Summary

Risk management is the framework that decides how much can be lost, how exposure is controlled and when trading should stop. It includes position sizing, invalidation, account limits, total exposure, execution conditions and review.

A trader does not need to predict every market movement. The essential goal is to keep losses controlled and behavior consistent enough to evaluate a strategy over a meaningful sample.

Core principle: Define risk first. Then decide whether the trade is worth taking.
