Trend Analysis

Learn how to identify bullish trends, bearish trends and ranging markets by reading the movement of price and its sequence of important highs and lows.

📈 Bullish Trend 📉 Bearish Trend ↔️ Range 🔄 Pullback 🎯 Trend Strength ⚠️ Trend Change

01. What Is Trend Analysis?

Trend analysis is the process of studying how price is moving over time and determining whether the market is generally moving upward, downward, or sideways.

The purpose is not to predict every candle. Instead, trend analysis helps us understand the current directional environment and organize the price action we are seeing.

SIMPLE DEFINITION

Trend analysis = studying the direction and behavior of price over a selected period and timeframe.

02. The Three Main Market Conditions

Before trying to trade a market, first determine what type of environment price is currently creating.

↑

Bullish Trend

Price generally moves upward and commonly creates Higher Highs and Higher Lows.

↓

Bearish Trend

Price generally moves downward and commonly creates Lower Highs and Lower Lows.

↔

Ranging Market

Price moves sideways between important upper and lower areas.

?

Transition

Price may be losing its previous directional structure and beginning to behave differently.

03. Bullish Trend

A bullish trend is a market condition where price generally moves upward. A common structural description is a sequence of Higher Highs and Higher Lows.

CORE IDEA

HH → HL → HH → HL

BULLISH TREND HH → HL → HH → HL

Notice that price does not move upward in a straight line. It makes advances and pullbacks. The important question is whether the pullbacks maintain the larger structural sequence.

04. Bearish Trend

A bearish trend is a market condition where price generally moves downward. A common structural description is a sequence of Lower Lows and Lower Highs.

CORE IDEA

LL → LH → LL → LH

BEARISH TREND LL → LH → LL → LH

05. Ranging Market

A ranging market is a condition where price repeatedly moves between an upper and lower area without producing a clear sustained directional trend.

SIDEWAYS MARKET RANGE HIGH ↔ RANGE LOW
⚠️ IMPORTANT

A market does not have to be trending at all times. Recognizing a range is just as important as recognizing a trend.

06. Trend Analysis + Market Structure

Market structure gives us one of the clearest ways to organize trend analysis.

Bullish

HH + HL sequence.

Bearish

LH + LL sequence.

Range

No clean sustained directional sequence.

🧠 REMEMBER

Trend analysis should not be based on one candle. Look at the sequence of meaningful price swings.

07. What Is a Pullback?

A pullback is a temporary movement against the current broader direction.

For example, during a bullish trend, price can temporarily move downward before continuing upward. During a bearish trend, price can temporarily move upward before continuing downward.

Bullish Trend Pullback

Main direction = upward. Temporary movement = downward.

Bearish Trend Pullback

Main direction = downward. Temporary movement = upward.

VERY IMPORTANT

A pullback is not automatically a trend reversal. The surrounding structure must be examined.

08. Bullish Trend With a Pullback

TREND + PULLBACK UP → DOWN → UP
🧠 KEY IDEA

Pullback ≠ automatic reversal. Always compare the pullback with the important market structure.

09. Understanding Trend Strength

Not every trend moves with the same strength. Some trends move quickly with relatively strong directional candles, while others move slowly with deeper and more frequent pullbacks.

Strong Movement

Price makes clear directional progress with relatively limited opposing movement.

Moderate Movement

Direction is visible, but pullbacks and pauses occur more frequently.

Weak / Unclear

Price makes little directional progress and may begin transitioning into a range.

⚠️ DO NOT JUDGE STRENGTH FROM ONE CANDLE

One large candle does not automatically mean that the entire market has become a strong trend. Examine the broader sequence.

10. What Makes a Trend Easier to Read?

  • Clear swing points. Important highs and lows are visually distinguishable.
  • Consistent direction. Price repeatedly makes structural progress in one direction.
  • Controlled pullbacks. Opposing moves do not immediately destroy the larger structure.
  • Less random overlap. Price is not constantly moving back and forth through the same area.
  • Timeframe consistency. The trend is understood in the context of the timeframe being analysed.

11. Trendlines as a Visual Tool

A trendline can be used as a visual aid to connect meaningful swing points. It can help organize the direction of price, but it should not replace actual market-structure analysis.

Rising Trendline

Often drawn beneath relevant rising lows in an upward-moving market.

Falling Trendline

Often drawn above relevant falling highs in a downward-moving market.

⚠️ REMEMBER

A trendline break by itself does not guarantee a reversal. Always examine the surrounding structure.

13. Higher Timeframe Trend

A higher timeframe gives a broader view of the market. It can help reduce the chance of confusing a small temporary movement with the larger directional environment.

Higher Timeframe

Shows broader swings and larger structural movement.

Lower Timeframe

Shows smaller movements that can occur inside the larger structure.

SIMPLE EXAMPLE

A market can be bullish on a higher timeframe while temporarily moving bearish on a lower timeframe. The lower-timeframe movement may simply be a pullback within the larger structure.

14. Multi-Timeframe Trend Analysis

When analysing multiple timeframes, start from the larger picture and then move toward the smaller picture.

01

Start With the Higher Timeframe

Determine the broad market condition.

02

Move to the Middle Timeframe

Study the intermediate structure and current movement.

03

Check the Lower Timeframe

Observe the smaller swings and pullbacks.

04

Compare the Structures

Determine whether the lower timeframe is continuing or temporarily moving against the larger structure.

15. How Can a Trend Begin to Change?

A trend does not necessarily change direction in one instant. Often the price behavior changes gradually.

  • Momentum slows. Price stops making the same level of directional progress.
  • Pullbacks become deeper. Opposing movements begin taking more of the previous move back.
  • Important structure is challenged. A key higher low or lower high may be tested or broken.
  • New structure begins forming. Price may start producing the opposite sequence.
  • Market can enter a range first. A trend may pause before a new directional trend develops.

16. Trend → Range → New Trend

A useful way to understand market transitions is to imagine that price can move from one condition into another instead of instantly switching from bullish to bearish.

MARKET TRANSITION TREND → RANGE → TREND
🧠 IMPORTANT

A period of consolidation or range can occur between directional trends. Do not force a trend label when price behavior is unclear.

17. Pullback vs Possible Reversal

Pullback

Price moves temporarily against the broader direction while the important structure remains intact.

Possible Reversal

Price begins breaking important structural points and may start forming the opposite sequence.

⚠️ DO NOT RUSH THE LABEL

A single bearish candle inside a bullish trend does not automatically create a bearish trend. Context matters.

18. Step-by-Step Trend Analysis

01

Choose the Timeframe

Decide which timeframe you are analysing before labeling the trend.

02

Zoom Out

Look at enough historical candles to understand the broader movement.

03

Mark Important Swings

Identify meaningful swing highs and swing lows.

04

Compare Highs

Are new highs becoming higher or lower?

05

Compare Lows

Are new lows becoming higher or lower?

06

Classify the Market

Bullish, bearish, ranging, or transitioning.

07

Recheck the Structure

Do not make the decision from only one candle or one small movement.

19. A Simple Top-Down Method

A beginner-friendly way to organize trend analysis is to work from the larger picture toward the smaller picture.

1. Direction

What is the broad market direction?

2. Structure

What are the important HH, HL, LH and LL points?

3. Current Phase

Is price trending, pulling back, ranging or transitioning?

20. Common Trend Analysis Mistakes

  • Calling every green candle bullish.
  • Calling every red candle bearish.
  • Ignoring market structure.
  • Looking at only one candle.
  • Ignoring the timeframe.
  • Confusing a pullback with a reversal.
  • Forcing a trend during a range.
  • Assuming a trend will continue forever.
  • Changing the trend label after every small movement.
  • Using trendlines without looking at price structure.

21. When the Trend Is Unclear

Sometimes the correct analysis is simply that the market is unclear. You do not need to force price into a bullish or bearish category.

ASK YOURSELF

Are the highs clearly rising? Are the lows clearly rising? Are both clearly falling? Or is price repeatedly moving through the same area?

GOOD HABIT

"No clear trend" is a valid observation.

22. Trend Analysis With Support and Resistance

Trend direction and important price zones can be studied together. A bullish market can still react at resistance, and a bearish market can still react at support.

Bullish Environment

Watch how price reacts around important support and previous swing lows.

Bearish Environment

Watch how price reacts around important resistance and previous swing highs.

CONNECTION

Trend tells you the broader directional environment. Support and resistance tell you where price may react.

23. Trend Analysis With Liquidity

Liquidity analysis can be used alongside trend analysis to study important areas around visible highs and lows.

Trend

Shows the broader directional behavior.

Structure

Shows the sequence of important highs and lows.

Liquidity

Helps study areas around obvious highs and lows.

24. How to Practise Trend Analysis

The best way to learn trend analysis is to practise identifying market conditions on historical charts without immediately worrying about taking a trade.

01

Open a Historical Chart

Choose a period where the future candles are not immediately visible if possible.

02

Hide the Future

Move through the chart gradually instead of looking at the completed movement.

03

Label the Structure

Mark HH, HL, LH and LL where appropriate.

04

Classify the Condition

Write bullish, bearish, range or transition.

05

Continue Forward

See whether the structure continued or changed.

06

Review Your Labels

Check whether you were labeling meaningful swings consistently rather than reacting to every small candle.

26. Quick Self-Test

Question 1

If price creates HH → HL → HH, what type of structure is this commonly describing?

Question 2

Question 3

ANSWERS

1. Bullish structure. 2. Bearish structure. 3. No. The surrounding structure must be examined.

🎯 Final Summary

Trend analysis is about understanding the broader directional behavior of price rather than trying to predict every individual candle.

A bullish market commonly creates Higher Highs and Higher Lows. A bearish market commonly creates Lower Highs and Lower Lows. A ranging market moves sideways without a clean sustained directional sequence.

Pullbacks are normal parts of trends. A pullback should not automatically be treated as a reversal. The surrounding market structure must be studied.

The most important skill is learning to recognize the market condition consistently and objectively before making any trading decision.

🧠 THE CORE LESSON

Start with the big picture → identify the important swings → compare HH/HL/LH/LL → classify the market → identify pullbacks or ranges → watch for structural changes.
