Introduction to Forex

The Forex market is the global marketplace for exchanging national currencies against one another. It operates 24 hours a day during the trading week, providing liquidity for international trade and opportunities for traders to profit from exchange rate fluctuations.

A complete beginner-friendly foundation for understanding the foreign exchange market before moving into currency pairs, charts, price action, strategies and risk management.

02 What Is Forex?

Forex is short for foreign exchange. It refers to the global market in which currencies are exchanged against one another.

Whenever one currency is exchanged for another, there is a foreign-exchange transaction involved. International companies may need foreign currency to pay suppliers. Travelers may exchange money when visiting another country. Financial institutions may exchange currencies as part of their operations, hedging or investment activities.

Retail traders participate for a different reason: they may attempt to benefit from changes in currency prices. A retail trader normally accesses the market through a broker or trading platform rather than directly participating in the entire institutional foreign-exchange network.

Diagram — The basic idea of foreign exchange

Forex is fundamentally about exchanging and comparing currencies. A currency is not quoted in isolation.

Key idea

The most important beginner concept is this: Forex is a comparison market. A currency pair tells you how one currency is valued relative to another currency.

03 Why Do People Exchange Currencies?

Currency exchange is not created only for trading. Foreign currencies are required because countries, companies, institutions and individuals interact across borders.

🌍 International Trade

An importer may need to pay a foreign supplier in the supplier's currency. An exporter may receive payment in a foreign currency and later convert it.

✈️ Travel

Travelers often exchange their home currency into the currency accepted in the destination country.

🏦 Financial Operations

Banks and financial institutions exchange currencies as part of international financial activity.

🛡️ Hedging

Businesses and institutions can use currency markets to manage exposure to changes in exchange rates.

📊 Investment

Some funds and institutions hold foreign assets and therefore have currency exposure.

💹 Speculation

Traders may attempt to profit from changes in exchange rates by taking positions in currency pairs.

Remember:

Currency exchange exists in the real economy first. Retail trading is only one part of the much larger foreign-exchange ecosystem.

04 Why Forex Uses Currency Pairs

You will almost never see a Forex price written as simply “EUR = 1.10” without another currency being specified. The market needs a reference currency because exchange rates describe relative value.

That is why currencies are written as pairs such as EUR/USD, GBP/USD or USD/JPY.

Diagram — Reading EUR/USD

The first currency is the base. The second currency is the quote. The number tells you how much quote currency is represented by one unit of base currency.

Memory trick

FIRST = BASE.
SECOND = QUOTE.

05 Base Currency and Quote Currency

Suppose EUR/USD is quoted at 1.1000. The simple educational interpretation is that one euro is being quoted as 1.1000 US dollars.

The number can change continuously as market participants change their willingness to exchange currencies. If the quote rises to 1.1050, the quoted value of one euro in US dollars has increased. If it falls to 1.0950, the quoted value has decreased.

Part Meaning Example: EUR/USD
Base currency The first currency in the pair. EUR
Quote currency The second currency used to express the value. USD
Exchange rate The quoted amount of quote currency for one unit of base currency. 1.1000 USD per EUR

06 What Does It Mean When a Pair Rises?

This is one of the most important ideas to understand before opening a chart.

If EUR/USD moves from 1.1000 to 1.1050, the pair has risen. In the quotation, one euro is now being expressed as more US dollars than before.

If EUR/USD moves from 1.1050 to 1.1000, the pair has fallen. One euro is now being expressed as fewer US dollars than before.

Diagram — Rising vs falling currency pair

Always interpret movement using the entire pair, not one currency in isolation.

Beginner mistake:

Do not say “EUR/USD is a currency.” EUR/USD is a currency pair representing the relative quotation between EUR and USD.

07 Buying and Selling a Currency Pair

Once you understand the pair, the idea of long and short positions becomes easier.

🟢 Long / Buy

A trader who buys EUR/USD is taking a position that benefits if the quoted price rises, before considering transaction costs and other factors.

🔴 Short / Sell

A trader who sells EUR/USD is taking a position that benefits if the quoted price falls, before considering transaction costs and other factors.

08 Who Participates in the Forex Market?

The foreign-exchange market contains participants with very different goals. Understanding this helps prevent the beginner mistake of thinking that every participant is simply trying to “make a trade.”

🏦 Banks

Banks facilitate currency transactions and participate in the broader institutional foreign-exchange market.

🏛️ Central Banks

Central banks conduct monetary policy and manage official reserves. Their decisions can influence currency expectations.

🏢 Businesses

International companies may exchange currencies to pay suppliers, receive revenue or manage currency exposure.

📈 Funds

Investment funds and other institutions can have currency exposure through investments and hedging.

🌍 Importers & Exporters

Cross-border businesses often need to convert one currency into another to conduct trade.

👤 Retail Traders

Individual traders generally access currency markets through a broker or trading platform.

09 Why Do Forex Prices Move?

Currency prices change because market participants constantly reassess information, expectations, risk and the relative attractiveness of holding one currency versus another.

Economic releases, interest-rate expectations, central-bank decisions, political developments, global risk sentiment and changing supply and demand can all influence the market.

Diagram — Simplified price-movement process
Important

A news headline does not mechanically guarantee a price direction. The market reacts to expectations, positioning, liquidity and how new information compares with what was already expected.

10 Forex as a Continuous Market

For learning purposes, it is useful to think of the market as a continuous process in which participants are willing to exchange at different prices. The quoted price changes as the balance of willing buyers and sellers changes.

This idea becomes very important later when you study candlesticks, market structure, support and resistance, liquidity and price action.

Diagram — Buyers and sellers create changing prices

This is a simplified teaching diagram. It should not be interpreted as saying that every price move is caused by one side “winning” in a simple way. Real market microstructure is more complex.

11 Bid and Ask: The Two-Sided Quote

A Forex quote normally has two sides: the Bid and the Ask.

The exact display and execution details can vary by broker and trading platform, but the basic concept is consistent: there is a price on one side of the market and another price on the other side.

Diagram — Simplified Bid / Ask quote

In the example above, the difference between 1.1001 and 1.0999 is the spread in price terms. The monetary effect of a spread depends on the instrument, position size and broker's specifications.

12 What Is the Spread?

The spread is the difference between the Bid and Ask prices.

Traders should understand that a position can have a small unfavorable price difference immediately after entry because the two sides of the market are different.

Simple formula

Spread = Ask − Bid

Term Simple meaning Example
Bid One side of the market quote. 1.0999
Ask The other side of the market quote. 1.1001
Spread Difference between Ask and Bid. 0.0002 in this example

13 A First Look at Pips

You will study pips in greater detail in the dedicated Pips and Points note. For now, understand that a pip is a commonly used unit for describing a small movement in a currency exchange rate.

The exact decimal convention can depend on the currency pair and market quotation. Some platforms also display fractional pip pricing, often called a pipette.

Diagram — Simplified price movement
Do not memorize money value yet.

A pip is a unit of price movement. Its monetary impact depends on position size and other instrument details.

14 Position Size and Lots

A trader's result is affected not only by the direction of the market but also by the size of the position.

In Forex, trade size is commonly described using lots. Brokers may support different contract specifications and lot sizes, so the exact meaning should always be checked against the instrument specification of the platform being used.

Position size

Describes how much market exposure the trader has.

Lot

A standard way of describing trade size under a particular contract specification.

Risk impact

Larger exposure can make the financial effect of a price movement larger.

Important:

Never choose a large position merely because the account balance appears large enough. Position sizing must be considered together with risk management.

15 What Is Leverage?

Leverage is a mechanism that can provide greater market exposure relative to the trader's own capital. The exact leverage available depends on the broker, jurisdiction, account type and instrument.

Leverage does not make the market safer. Greater exposure can magnify the financial effect of price movements relative to the trader's own capital.

Diagram — Why leverage requires caution

The diagram is deliberately simplified. Real margin and leverage calculations depend on contract specifications, instrument price, account currency and broker rules.

16 Forex Sessions: A First Look

Forex activity is commonly discussed using major financial centers and trading sessions such as Asian, London and New York sessions. The exact clock times depend on the time zone and daylight-saving rules being used.

You will study sessions in detail in the dedicated Market Sessions note. For now, understand that market activity can change during the day and that periods of overlapping major centers can be especially important for liquidity and volatility.

Diagram — Simplified session sequence

17 What Forex Is Not

  • Forex is not guaranteed income. Market prices can move against a position.
  • Forex is not a prediction machine. Analysis creates a framework for decisions; it does not remove uncertainty.
  • Forex is not only charts. Economic conditions, institutions, liquidity and expectations also matter.
  • Forex is not the same as a single currency. A quoted pair always contains two currencies.
  • Leverage is not free profit. Greater exposure can increase the effect of adverse price movement.

18 Common Beginner Mistakes

❌ Reading the pair backwards

Always identify the first currency as the base and the second as the quote.

❌ Thinking a currency moves alone

Exchange rates describe relative value between two currencies.

❌ Ignoring Bid and Ask

A market quote has two sides. The difference between them is the spread.

❌ Confusing pips with money

A pip describes price movement. Monetary impact depends on position size and instrument details.

❌ Starting with leverage

Beginners should understand exposure and risk before thinking about increasing market exposure.

❌ Jumping directly to strategies

A strategy is easier to understand when the market's basic language is already clear.

In EUR/USD, which currency is the base currency?

Answer

Answer

The pair rose.

Answer

Question 4

Answer

No. Education can improve understanding, but market outcomes remain uncertain and losses are possible.

23 Final Summary

Forex is the global foreign-exchange market in which currencies are exchanged and valued relative to one another.

The most important foundation is learning to read a currency pair correctly. In EUR/USD, EUR is the base currency and USD is the quote currency. A quoted price tells you how much quote currency is represented by one unit of the base currency.

Once that idea is clear, price movement becomes easier to understand. A rising EUR/USD quotation means the quoted value of EUR in USD has increased. A falling quotation means it has decreased.

You also now have a first understanding of Bid, Ask, spread, pips, position size, leverage, market participants, sessions and the basic reasons prices can change.

One sentence to remember:

Forex is a market where the value of one currency is expressed relative to another currency.
