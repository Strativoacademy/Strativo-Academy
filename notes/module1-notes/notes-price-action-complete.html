SECTION 01

What Price Actually Is

Price is the current market valuation at which transactions can occur. It changes continuously as orders interact and participants agree to transact at different prices.

Simple Explanation

Think of price as the constantly changing point at which buyers and sellers are willing to transact. The chart is not the market itself. A chart is a visual representation of the market's historical price changes.

Why This Matters

  • A candle is a summary of price movement during a chosen period.
  • A series of candles shows movement.
  • Repeated movement creates swing points.
  • Swing points create structure.
Price → Movement → Swings → Structure → Context
SECTION 02

Why Price Moves

Price moves because market participants continuously submit, cancel and execute orders at different prices.

The Basic Idea

When aggressive buying consumes available selling interest at one level, participants may need to transact at higher prices. When aggressive selling consumes available buying interest, transactions can move toward lower prices.

BUYING AND SELLING PRESSURE
SECTION 03

Buyers vs Sellers

Every executed transaction has a buyer and a seller. Price movement reflects the changing willingness of participants to transact at available prices.

🟢 Buyers

Buyers generally want to acquire an asset. When buying pressure becomes more aggressive relative to available selling interest, price can move upward.

🔴 Sellers

Sellers generally want to dispose of an asset. When selling pressure becomes more aggressive relative to available buying interest, price can move downward.

Important

It is too simplistic to say that every green candle means buyers are winning or every red candle means sellers are winning. Candle color is only one piece of information. Location, size, wicks and following price action matter.

SECTION 04

How an Auction / Market Forms

Financial markets can be understood as continuous auctions where participants negotiate through orders and transactions.

Simple Auction Model

  1. Participants submit orders.
  2. Transactions occur where buyers and sellers meet.
  3. When interest changes, price searches for another level.
  4. Repeated transactions create the price history shown on a chart.
PRICE AS AN AUCTION SEARCH

Body and Wicks

The body shows the open-to-close range. Wicks show the prices reached outside the body during the candle period.

BODY SIZE AND WICK SIZE

Reading the Candle

A large body can show strong directional movement during the candle. A long wick can show that price traveled beyond the body before closing elsewhere. Neither feature should be interpreted without location and surrounding structure.

Entry Logic

An entry should be the result of a defined scenario rather than an emotional reaction to a fast candle.

Example Logical Sequence

  1. Identify market context.
  2. Identify structure.
  3. Mark important area.
  4. Wait for price to reach the area.
  5. Observe the reaction.
  6. Define invalidation.
  7. Only then consider an entry.

SECTION 34

Stop-Loss Logic

A stop-loss should be connected to the point where the trade idea becomes invalid, not simply placed at an arbitrary distance.

STRUCTURE-BASED INVALIDATION

Risk Comes First

The location of the stop determines the amount of price movement that must be allowed. Position size should then be calculated from the amount of money the trader is willing to risk.

SECTION 38

Common Mistakes

❌ Starting With Signals

Learning entries before understanding price itself creates confusion.

❌ Calling Every Tiny High an HH

Noise is not automatically meaningful structure.

❌ Treating Zones as Exact Lines

Price can move through an area before reacting.

❌ Chasing Candles

A fast candle is not automatically an entry signal.

❌ Ignoring Context

The same candle can mean different things in different locations.

❌ Ignoring Risk

A good analysis can still produce a losing trade.

SECTION 41

Advanced: Internal vs. External Structure

Smart Money Concepts (SMC) often refine traditional price action by distinguishing between major swing points (External) and the smaller movements within that leg (Internal).

A common mistake is treating every minor pullback as a major trend change. External Structure frames the macro context, while Internal Structure guides short-term order flow. Breaking internal structure does not necessarily reverse the external trend.

EXTERNAL MACRO TREND VS INTERNAL PULLBACKS
SECTION 42

Fair Value Gaps (FVG) / Imbalance

An FVG occurs during rapid price expansion when aggressive orders leave a gap between the wicks of a 3-candle sequence, indicating inefficiency in the market.

Bullish FVG

The gap formed between the high wick of Candle 1 and the low wick of Candle 3. Price often returns here later to rebalance the orders.

Bearish FVG

The gap formed between the low wick of Candle 1 and the high wick of Candle 3 during a fast downward move.

BULLISH FAIR VALUE GAP (IMBALANCE)
SECTION 43

Order Blocks (OB)

An Order Block is typically the last opposite-colored candle before a strong institutional impulse that breaks structure and leaves a Fair Value Gap behind.

Order blocks represent footprint areas where large market participants likely accumulated their positions. A valid OB must result in a structural break (BOS or CHoCH) and have unmitigated imbalance (FVG) attached to it.

VALID BULLISH ORDER BLOCK REFINEMENT
SECTION 44

Inducement (IDM) & Liquidity

Inducement refers to short-term highs or lows designed to trigger early breakout or pullback traders. SMC traders wait for these levels to be swept before entering.

The market needs liquidity to fill large orders. By tricking early buyers/sellers into the market at an obvious short-term level (the Inducement), large players can sweep their stops to fuel the true institutional move from a deeper Order Block.

BEARISH INDUCEMENT SWEEP
SECTION 45

Premium & Discount Pricing

Markets move between extremes. Using a 50% equilibrium line, traders identify whether an asset is currently expensive (Premium) or cheap (Discount).

Buying in Discount

Only look for long setups (Bullish OBs, support) when price is in the lower 50% of the current dealing range.

Selling in Premium

Only look for short setups (Bearish OBs, resistance) when price is in the upper 50% of the current dealing range.

RANGE EQUILIBRIUM
SECTION 46

AMD (Accumulation, Manipulation, Distribution)

Also known as the Power of 3 (PO3), this concept frames how daily price action often behaves across global trading sessions.

A
Accumulation (Asian Session)Price builds liquidity in a tight, quiet consolidation range.
M
Manipulation (London Session)A false breakout sweeping Asian liquidity to trap retail traders.
D
Distribution (NY Session)The true directional expansion of the day.

Complete Price Action & SMC Summary

Price action starts before the chart pattern. First understand what price is and why markets move. Then understand buyers, sellers, the auction process and bid/ask. From there, understand how raw price changes become candles.

TICK → CANDLE → SWING → HH/HL/LH/LL → MARKET STRUCTURE

Once classical structure is understood, advance your knowledge by integrating SMC concepts. Recognize when structure breaks are just Inducements, locate unmitigated Order Blocks with Imbalances (FVGs), and trade from Premium or Discount extremes instead of random mid-range levels.

CONTEXT → STRUCTURE → PREMIUM/DISCOUNT → LIQUIDITY SWEEP → IMBALANCE/OB → REACTION → ENTRY

The objective is not to predict every candle. The objective is to build a logical process for reading the chart, defining a scenario and controlling risk.

The Core Learning Sequence

  1. Understand price, auction mechanics, and buyers vs sellers.
  2. Understand OHLC candle formation.
  3. Identify swing highs and swing lows.
  4. Read external vs internal market structure.
  5. Identify important liquidity pools (Equal highs/lows, Inducement).
  6. Identify FVGs and Order Blocks.
  7. Determine Premium and Discount zones.
  8. Wait for price to sweep liquidity and react at an OB.
  9. Build an entry and risk plan.
  10. Practice reading charts repeatedly.

Educational Disclaimer

These notes are for educational purposes only. Price action and SMC analysis cannot guarantee future market movements. Financial markets involve risk. Students should understand risk management and practice concepts in a controlled environment before considering real-money trading.

The Market as an Auction

Understanding price discovery

One useful way to understand markets is to think of them as an ongoing auction. Buyers and sellers continuously interact to find prices where transactions can occur.

Simple Market Auction

🧠 Remember

Think of price as a story created by the interaction between buyers and sellers. Candlesticks are simply the visual record of that story.

SECTION 06 (DEEP DIVE)

Reading Candle Strength

Body, wick and closing position

Large Body

A large candle body means the opening and closing prices were relatively far apart. It can indicate strong movement during that period.

Small Body

A small body means the open and close were relatively close together. It may indicate hesitation, balance or temporary uncertainty.

Long Wick

A wick shows that price travelled away from the body during the candle but later moved back. A long wick can provide evidence of rejection, but the location is extremely important.

Body Size and Wick Examples
SECTION 07 (DEEP DIVE)

Buying and Selling Pressure

Reading the battle inside price

Price action becomes much easier to understand when you stop thinking only in terms of "green candle" and "red candle". Instead ask:

Who is stronger?

Are buyers or sellers currently pushing price?

How strong?

Is the movement fast and large or slow and weak?

Where?

Is the movement happening at an important area?

Example:

A strong bullish candle in the middle of nowhere gives less context than a strong bullish reaction from a well-defined support area.
