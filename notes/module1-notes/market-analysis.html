01What Is Market Analysis?

Market analysis is the process of studying information that helps a trader understand the current market environment and make decisions according to a defined strategy.

Simple Example:

42Complete Analysis Workflow

  1. Identify the pair and timeframe.
  2. Check broad fundamental context and scheduled events.
  3. Review sentiment where relevant.
  4. Study price structure and important areas.
  5. Wait for the strategy's exact setup.
  6. Define invalidation and risk.
  7. Execute only when all required conditions exist.
  8. Record the decision for later review.
Core rule: Never invent a reason after the trade has already happened.

43Worked Examples

44Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Believing one analysis method predicts perfectly.
  • Adding indicators without knowing their purpose.
  • Treating news as a guaranteed direction.
  • Using sentiment as an automatic buy/sell signal.
  • Ignoring timeframes.
  • Using hindsight in backtesting.
  • Changing rules after losses.
  • Confusing confluence with certainty.
  • Using too many conflicting filters.

45Complete Analysis Checklist

  • I know which analysis methods my strategy uses.
  • I know what question each method answers.
  • I defined the timeframe.
  • I checked relevant economic events.
  • I understand sentiment is context.
  • I identified technical structure.
  • I know my exact setup conditions.
  • I define risk before entry.
  • I do not use future information when reviewing past trades.
  • I record analysis consistently.
  • I accept that strong analysis can still be wrong.

46Final Summary

Technical analysis focuses on price and market data. Fundamental analysis focuses on economic and policy forces. Sentiment analysis focuses on market mood and positioning.

These approaches can be used separately or together. The important part is to define how they fit the strategy, test the rules consistently, and control risk.

Remember: Analysis improves understanding; it does not remove uncertainty.
