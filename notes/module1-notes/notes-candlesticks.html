01. What Is a Candlestick?

A candlestick is a visual representation of price movement during one selected period. On a 5-minute chart, one completed candle represents five minutes of price activity; on a 15-minute chart, it represents fifteen minutes.

Every completed candle contains four core values: Open, High, Low and Close (OHLC). A candle compresses a period of price activity into these values, allowing the trader to study what happened without viewing every individual price movement.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

OHLC is the foundation. Learn what price did before trying to predict what price may do next.

02. OHLC — Open, High, Low and Close

Open is the price at which the period began. High is the highest price reached during the period. Low is the lowest price reached. Close is the price at which the period ended.

The relationship between Open and Close determines whether a completed candle is bullish or bearish. High and Low show the full extremes reached during the period.

OPEN

Beginning price of the selected period.

HIGH

Highest price reached during the period.

LOW

Lowest price reached during the period.

CLOSE

Ending price of the selected period.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Open = start • High = highest • Low = lowest • Close = finish.

03. Complete Candle Anatomy

The candle body represents the distance between Open and Close. The upper wick represents the price movement above the body, while the lower wick represents the price movement below the body.

The full candle range is the distance between High and Low. The body and wicks should always be studied together because they describe different parts of the same price movement.

CANDLE ANATOMY OHLC + BODY + WICKS
REMEMBER

Body shows Open-to-Close movement; wicks show the additional travel beyond the body.

04. Bullish and Bearish Candles

A bullish candle closes above its Open. A bearish candle closes below its Open. Chart colours are configurable, so always use Open and Close rather than relying only on colour.

A bullish candle means price finished higher than it started for that period. A bearish candle means price finished lower than it started. Neither guarantees the direction of the next candle.

BULLISH vs BEARISH OPEN / CLOSE
REMEMBER

Close > Open = bullish. Close < Open = bearish.

05. Candle Body Size

A small body means Open and Close are relatively close. A large body means the Open-to-Close movement is relatively large compared with nearby candles.

Body size can describe momentum or participation in a completed period, but a large candle is not automatically a continuation signal. Compare it with surrounding candles and location.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Large body describes a strong completed Open-to-Close move; it does not guarantee continuation.

06. Upper and Lower Wicks

Wicks show that price travelled beyond the body before the candle closed. A long upper wick documents movement to higher prices followed by a return from that extreme. A long lower wick documents movement to lower prices followed by a return.

A wick can provide a rejection clue, but it is not a guaranteed reversal signal. Location, structure and follow-through must be considered.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

A wick tells you where price travelled and where it finished relative to that extreme.

07. Candle Range

The total candle range is the distance between High and Low. It includes the body plus the upper and lower wick areas.

Comparing ranges across nearby candles can help students recognise periods of expansion and contraction.

TOTAL RANGE = HIGH − LOW
CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Range = High − Low.

08. Close Position

Where a candle closes inside its total range can provide additional information. A candle that closes near its high finished relatively close to the upper extreme; a candle that closes near its low finished relatively close to the lower extreme.

Close location should be studied together with wick size and surrounding price behaviour rather than treated as a standalone prediction.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Always ask: did price finish near the high, near the low, or around the middle of its range?

09. Doji

A Doji occurs when Open and Close are very close relative to the candle's range. It can show that the period finished near where it began.

Doji candles can represent uncertainty or balance during that period, but a Doji does not automatically mean a reversal. Its meaning depends strongly on location and what happens afterward.

STUDY RULE

Describe the candle first. Interpret it second. Never let the pattern name replace the actual price information.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Doji = Open and Close are close; context decides how important it is.

10. Spinning Top

A spinning top generally has a relatively small body with meaningful upper and lower wicks. It can show that price travelled in both directions but finished near its starting area.

Like a Doji, it should not be treated as an automatic reversal signal. Study the preceding trend, location and next price response.

STUDY RULE

Describe the candle first. Interpret it second. Never let the pattern name replace the actual price information.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Small body + noticeable wicks = study the struggle, not an automatic trade.

11. Marubozu and Strong Directional Candles

A Marubozu-style candle has a very large body with little or no visible wick compared with the body. It shows that Open-to-Close movement dominated the candle's range.

A strong directional candle can demonstrate strong movement during that period, but entering late simply because a candle is large can expose a trader to pullbacks. Context and risk management remain necessary.

STUDY RULE

Describe the candle first. Interpret it second. Never let the pattern name replace the actual price information.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Strong candle = strong completed movement, not a promise about the next candle.

12. Small-Body and Large-Body Comparison

Comparing body size with nearby candles is more useful than judging a candle as large or small in isolation.

Expansion can appear when candle bodies and ranges grow. Contraction can appear when bodies and ranges shrink. These observations help students study changing market behaviour.

STUDY RULE

Describe the candle first. Interpret it second. Never let the pattern name replace the actual price information.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Compare candles with their neighbours instead of using an absolute size rule.

13. Hammer

A Hammer is commonly described as a small body near the upper portion of the candle with a relatively long lower wick and a small upper wick. It is traditionally studied after a decline or near a relevant support area.

The long lower wick shows that price travelled lower and then recovered toward the close. A Hammer is not automatically a buy signal. Students should study location, preceding movement and confirmation.

13. HAMMER SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Hammer shape alone is not enough; location and follow-through matter.

14. Inverted Hammer

An Inverted Hammer has a small body near the lower portion of the candle with a relatively long upper wick. It is traditionally studied after a decline.

The upper wick shows that price moved higher during the period but did not maintain that extreme. A later bullish response can provide more information than the candle shape alone.

14. INVERTED HAMMER SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Study the attempted upward movement and the response that follows.

15. Hanging Man

A Hanging Man has a small body near the upper part of the candle with a relatively long lower wick and is traditionally studied after an advance.

The shape resembles a Hammer, which demonstrates why context is essential. The same general shape can receive a different interpretation depending on the preceding market movement.

15. HANGING MAN SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Same shape can have different interpretations depending on location and prior trend.

16. Shooting Star

A Shooting Star has a small body near the lower portion of the candle with a relatively long upper wick and is traditionally studied after an advance.

It shows that price reached higher levels but returned toward the lower part of the candle. A trader should still wait for context and subsequent behaviour rather than assuming an immediate reversal.

16. SHOOTING STAR SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Long upper wick after an advance can be a rejection clue, not a guaranteed sell signal.

17. Pin Bar / Rejection Candle

A pin-bar style candle is commonly used to describe a candle with a relatively long wick and a smaller body. Traders often study the rejected side and the candle's closing position.

The useful learning point is not the name itself but the sequence: price travelled into an area, failed to maintain that extreme, and closed away from it. Confirmation and context remain important.

17. PIN BAR / REJECTION CANDLE SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Read the event: approach → extreme → failure to hold → close → follow-through.

18. Bullish Engulfing

A Bullish Engulfing pattern is commonly formed by a bearish candle followed by a bullish candle whose real body covers the prior bearish real body under the conventional definition.

Students should study where it occurs, what the preceding trend was, and whether price follows through afterward. The pattern is not a guaranteed reversal.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

18. BULLISH ENGULFING SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Pattern + location + preceding movement + confirmation.

19. Bearish Engulfing

A Bearish Engulfing pattern is commonly formed by a bullish candle followed by a bearish candle whose real body covers the prior bullish real body under the conventional definition.

The pattern can show a strong change in the Open-to-Close relationship across the two candles, but its significance depends on market context.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

19. BEARISH ENGULFING SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Do not treat engulfing as a signal without studying location and structure.

20. Bullish Harami

A Bullish Harami is commonly described as a larger bearish candle followed by a smaller bullish candle whose body is contained within the previous candle's real body.

It can represent a change in short-term pressure, but students should study the preceding decline, relevant location and confirmation.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

20. BULLISH HARAMI SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

A smaller second body inside the prior body describes contraction; context determines interpretation.

21. Bearish Harami

A Bearish Harami is commonly described as a larger bullish candle followed by a smaller bearish candle whose body is contained within the previous candle's real body.

The pattern can show a pause or change in short-term pressure after an advance, but it is not automatically a reversal.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

21. BEARISH HARAMI SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Study the change in candle body behaviour and what follows.

22. Piercing Pattern

The Piercing Pattern is a two-candle bullish reversal formation traditionally studied after a decline. A bearish candle is followed by a bullish candle that opens lower and closes substantially into the prior bearish candle's body without fully closing above it.

Exact textbook rules can vary between sources and markets, so students should focus on the underlying behaviour: a strong bearish period is followed by a meaningful bullish recovery.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

22. PIERCING PATTERN SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

The important event is a significant recovery into the previous bearish body.

23. Dark Cloud Cover

Dark Cloud Cover is a two-candle bearish formation traditionally studied after an advance. A bullish candle is followed by a bearish candle that opens higher and closes substantially into the prior bullish candle's body.

The pattern describes a loss of upward control during the two-candle sequence. Context and confirmation remain necessary.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

23. DARK CLOUD COVER SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

The important event is a strong bearish recovery into the previous bullish body.

24. Tweezer Top and Tweezer Bottom

Tweezer patterns involve two candles that test a similar extreme. A Tweezer Top is traditionally studied around similar highs, while a Tweezer Bottom is studied around similar lows.

The repeated extreme can be useful information, but the level itself and the subsequent response are more important than the pattern name.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

24. TWEEZER TOP AND TWEEZER BOTTOM SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Repeated testing of an extreme is a price event; study what happens next.

25. Morning Star

A Morning Star is a three-candle bullish reversal formation traditionally studied after a decline: a strong bearish candle, a small middle candle showing hesitation or transition, and a strong bullish candle that recovers into the first candle's body.

The pattern is useful for studying a transition from strong selling to hesitation and then stronger buying, but context and confirmation still matter.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

25. MORNING STAR SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Three-candle story: pressure → pause → response.

26. Evening Star

An Evening Star is a three-candle bearish reversal formation traditionally studied after an advance: a strong bullish candle, a small middle candle, and a strong bearish candle that moves substantially into the first candle's body.

It can describe a transition from buying pressure to hesitation and then stronger selling pressure.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

26. EVENING STAR SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Three-candle story: pressure → pause → response in the opposite direction.

27. Three White Soldiers

Three White Soldiers describes a sequence of three strong bullish candles, commonly with each candle opening within or near the previous body and closing progressively higher.

The sequence can show sustained upward pressure, but students should consider whether price is already extended and where the sequence appears.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

27. THREE WHITE SOLDIERS SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Repeated bullish closes can describe sustained pressure, not guaranteed future continuation.

28. Three Black Crows

Three Black Crows describes a sequence of three strong bearish candles, commonly with each candle opening within or near the previous body and closing progressively lower.

The sequence can show sustained downward pressure, but location and extension matter.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

28. THREE BLACK CROWS SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Repeated bearish closes can describe sustained pressure, not a guaranteed next move.

29. Three Inside Up and Three Inside Down

Three Inside Up and Three Inside Down are three-candle formations built around a Harami-type first two candles followed by a confirming third candle.

The educational value is understanding the progression from a large directional candle to contraction and then a directional response.

⚠️ PATTERN WARNING

A textbook pattern is not a guaranteed trade signal. Study trend, location, structure and confirmation.

29. THREE INSIDE UP AND THREE INSIDE DOWN SHAPE + CONTEXT
REMEMBER

Large candle → contraction → confirmation.

30. Candle Rejection

Rejection is best understood as a sequence rather than a candle name. Price reaches an area, travels beyond or into an extreme, fails to hold that price, and closes away from the extreme.

A stronger rejection study includes the market location, prior structure, candle close and subsequent follow-through.

CONTEXT MATTERS LOCATION + CANDLE + RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Touch → excursion → failure to hold → close → response.

31. Breakout Candle

A breakout occurs when price moves beyond a previously watched level or zone. A candle that closes beyond the area can provide more information than a temporary intrabar spike through it.

Students should distinguish a genuine sustained break from a temporary move that returns inside the zone.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Breakout study = level → move through → close → follow-through.

32. Fake Breakout

A fake breakout occurs when price moves beyond a level or zone but fails to sustain the move and returns back through the area.

A false break can leave a wick beyond the level, but the wick alone is not enough to classify the event. Study the close and subsequent movement.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Temporary break + failure to hold + return = study as a possible false breakout.

33. Breakout and Retest

After a breakout, price may return toward the broken area. This is often called a retest. A retest is not guaranteed and the old level does not always behave perfectly as new support or resistance.

The important learning sequence is break → return → reaction → continuation or failure.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Do not assume every breakout must retest.

34. Candle Sequences

Reading sequences is more informative than memorising isolated shapes. A series can show expansion, contraction, acceleration, hesitation, rejection or continuation.

Ask what changed from one candle to the next: body size, range, closing position, wick behaviour and direction.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Read the story across candles, not just the name of one candle.

35. Candle Momentum

Repeated strong directional candles can describe momentum during the observed period. Smaller overlapping candles can describe a pause or contraction.

Momentum can weaken even while price continues in the same direction, so students should study the sequence rather than assuming continuation forever.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Momentum is observed through behaviour across time, not a single colour.

36. Candle + Market Structure

Candles become more useful when connected with swing highs, swing lows and market structure. A rejection candle near a higher-timeframe support area inside an uptrend is a different learning situation from the same candle inside a random range.

Market structure will be studied in greater depth in its dedicated lesson. Here, the goal is simply to learn that candles are evidence inside a larger price story.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Candle → location → structure → interpretation.

37. Candle + Support and Resistance

A candle near a previously identified support or resistance zone can provide information about how price responds to that area.

Support and resistance are zones rather than magical exact lines. Students should study repeated reactions and the behaviour of price around the zone.

CONTEXT MATTERS LOCATION + CANDLE + RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Location gives the candle meaning; the candle does not create the location.

38. Candle + Trend and Pullback

In a directional market, price can move, pause and pull back before continuing or changing direction. Candles inside a pullback should be interpreted relative to the larger movement.

Do not treat every bearish candle inside an uptrend as a trend reversal or every bullish candle inside a downtrend as a reversal.

CONTEXT MATTERS LOCATION + CANDLE + RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Always compare the local candle with the larger price movement.

39. Candle + Timeframe

The same market can produce different candle structures on different timeframes. A 5-minute candle compresses five minutes of information, while a 1-hour candle compresses sixty minutes.

A pattern on a lower timeframe should not automatically override the larger timeframe context.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Timeframe changes the amount of price information compressed into each candle.

40. Candle Confirmation

Confirmation means looking for additional evidence after an observation. Depending on the strategy, this may involve a close beyond an area, follow-through, structure change or a successful retest.

Confirmation does not remove uncertainty. It simply provides additional information before a decision.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Observation is not confirmation. Confirmation is additional evidence.

41. Candle Patterns Are Not Guarantees

No candlestick pattern guarantees what the market will do next. The same pattern can fail because market conditions, liquidity, volatility, news and structure change.

Patterns are tools for describing historical price behaviour. They should be combined with a complete trading plan and risk management.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

A pattern is evidence, not certainty.

42. Common Beginner Mistakes

Common errors include trading every Hammer, buying every bullish engulfing, selling every Shooting Star, assuming every Doji means reversal, ignoring location, ignoring timeframe, and entering because a candle looks strong.

Another major mistake is memorising names without understanding OHLC, body, wick, range and context.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

If you cannot explain what happened in the candle, do not rely on its pattern name.

43. Practical Candle Reading Process

Use a consistent reading process: identify the timeframe, read OHLC, inspect body and wicks, measure the candle's relative size, identify the location, inspect preceding candles, identify structure, and then observe follow-through.

This process keeps the student focused on evidence rather than prediction.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Timeframe → OHLC → anatomy → location → context → response.

44. Candle Study Exercises

Practice on historical charts without immediately placing trades. Pick random completed candles and write their Open, High, Low and Close relationships. Then identify body size, wick size, close location and surrounding structure.

Next, hide the candles to the right and make a description of what is visible. Then reveal the next candles and compare your interpretation with what actually happened. This develops observation without pretending that patterns predict the future.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Study first. Predict later. Review what actually happened.

45. Final Revision Checklist

Before moving forward, the student should be able to identify OHLC, body, wicks and range; classify bullish and bearish candles; explain Doji, Hammer, Shooting Star and major multi-candle patterns; and describe rejection, breakout, fake breakout, retest and candle sequences.

The student should also understand the central rule: candle appearance is only one piece of information. Location, structure, timeframe and follow-through matter.

CANDLE SEQUENCE EXPANSION → PAUSE → RESPONSE
REMEMBER

Master the basics before memorising more pattern names.

🎯 Final Candlestick Framework

Use this order whenever you study a candle:

1. TIMEFRAME

What period does this candle represent?

2. OHLC

Where did it open, travel and close?

3. ANATOMY

How large is the body? Where are the wicks?

4. LOCATION

Is price near support, resistance, structure or a range?

5. CONTEXT

What happened before this candle?

6. RESPONSE

What happens after the candle?

CORE RULE

Never treat a candlestick pattern as a guaranteed prediction. Candles describe completed price behaviour. Your job as a student is to understand the behaviour, location, context and follow-through.
