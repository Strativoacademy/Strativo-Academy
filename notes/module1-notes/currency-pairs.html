Currency Pairs

Forex trading always involves trading currencies in pairs, grouped into majors, minors, and exotics. The price of a pair reflects the current exchange rate, showing exactly how much of the quote currency is required to buy one unit of the base currency.

Build a complete foundation for understanding Forex currency pairs. Learn what a pair actually represents, how the base and quote currencies work, how to read prices, how bid and ask prices interact, how majors, minors and exotics differ, and how currency-pair structure affects the way traders read and plan the market.

01What Is a Currency Pair?

A currency pair is the way the Forex market expresses the value of one currency relative to another currency. Forex does not normally quote a currency in isolation. Instead, one currency is compared directly with another.

When you see EUR/USD, you are not simply seeing the name of a product called "Euro Dollar." You are looking at a relationship between two currencies. The euro is being valued relative to the U.S. dollar.

This relative structure is the foundation of Forex. The market is constantly comparing one currency with another as participants respond to economic information, interest-rate expectations, central-bank decisions, trade flows, risk sentiment, capital flows, liquidity and changing expectations.

Definition

A currency pair represents the amount of the quote currency required to represent one unit of the base currency.

Diagram — The Basic Structure of a Currency Pair

The first currency is the base; the second currency is the quote.

02Base Currency and Quote Currency

Every currency pair has two sides. The first currency is the base currency. The second currency is the quote currency.

1️⃣ Base Currency

The currency written on the left side. A quoted price tells you how much of the second currency is needed for one unit of the first.

2️⃣ Quote Currency

The currency written on the right side. It is the currency used to express the value of one unit of the base currency.

Easy memory rule

LEFT = BASE. RIGHT = QUOTE.

Example: GBP/USD

In GBP/USD, GBP is the base currency and USD is the quote currency. If the market shows 1.2500, the basic interpretation is that one British pound is being valued at 1.2500 U.S. dollars.

Example: USD/JPY

In USD/JPY, USD is the base currency and JPY is the quote currency. If the market shows 150.00, the basic interpretation is that one U.S. dollar is being valued at approximately 150 Japanese yen.

03How to Read a Currency-Pair Price

The price of a currency pair answers a relative question: "How much quote currency is needed for one unit of base currency?"

Diagram — Reading EUR/USD = 1.1000

04Why Does Forex Use Pairs?

A currency has no single universal price. Its value is always relative to something else. One U.S. dollar can be worth a certain amount of euros, yen, pounds or Swiss francs, and those relationships can all change.

Forex therefore organizes the market around relative value. When one side becomes stronger relative to the other, the quoted pair changes.

🌍 Relative value

Currency value is expressed relative to another currency.

⚖️ Comparison

A pair lets the market continuously compare two currencies.

📈 Price movement

The pair rises or falls as that relative relationship changes.

05What Does It Mean When a Pair Goes Up?

If EUR/USD rises from 1.1000 to 1.1050, the euro has become more valuable relative to the U.S. dollar over that observed move, or equivalently, the dollar has weakened relative to the euro in that pair.

The safest way to think about a rising pair is not simply "EUR is strong." The precise statement is that EUR has strengthened relative to USD within the EUR/USD relationship.

Diagram — Rising EUR/USD
Remember

Pair rises → base currency gains relative value against quote currency. Pair falls → base currency loses relative value against quote currency.

06What Does It Mean When a Pair Goes Down?

If EUR/USD falls from 1.1000 to 1.0950, one euro is now being valued at fewer U.S. dollars than before.

In relative terms, EUR has weakened against USD in the observed move, or USD has strengthened relative to EUR.

Simple rule

When reading any pair, identify the base currency first. Then ask: Is the base becoming more or less valuable relative to the quote?

The term major currency pairs is commonly used for the most heavily traded currency combinations, especially pairs involving the U.S. dollar and other major currencies.

Important terminology

Lists of "major pairs" can vary slightly by source. The important concept is that majors generally refer to highly traded currency combinations, with USD playing a central role.

09Minor / Cross Currency Pairs

A currency cross is a pair that does not directly include the U.S. dollar. Common examples include EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY and GBP/JPY.

EUR/GBP

Euro relative to pound sterling.

EUR/JPY

Euro relative to Japanese yen.

GBP/JPY

Pound sterling relative to Japanese yen.

Crosses are important because traders can analyze relationships between currencies without directly trading a USD pair.

10Exotic Currency Pairs

Exotic pairs generally combine a major currency with the currency of an emerging or smaller economy. Examples can include USD/TRY, USD/ZAR and USD/MXN.

Exotic markets can have different liquidity, spreads, volatility and execution characteristics compared with highly traded major pairs.

💧 Liquidity

Some exotic pairs may have less market depth than major pairs.

↔️ Spread

Spreads can be wider, particularly under certain market conditions.

⚡ Volatility

Price behavior can be materially different from major pairs.

11Why Is USD So Important?

The U.S. dollar has a central role in global finance and is present in a large share of foreign-exchange transactions. This makes USD pairs especially important for understanding Forex market relationships.

USD is also widely used in international trade, reserves, financing and financial markets. Changes in U.S. interest-rate expectations and economic conditions can therefore influence many currency relationships.

Foundation idea

When studying a USD pair, do not analyze only the chart. Understand that the pair is a relationship involving both currencies.

12Direct and Indirect Quote Thinking

A quote can be understood by asking which currency is being priced in terms of which other currency. This becomes particularly important when converting between currencies or comparing quotations.

Instead of trying to memorize terminology first, build the habit of reading the actual pair: identify the base, identify the quote, and interpret the numerical price.

EUR/USD

How many USD units represent one EUR?

USD/JPY

How many JPY units represent one USD?

13Bid and Ask Prices

A Forex platform normally shows at least two relevant prices: bid and ask.

  • Bid: the price at which the market can buy the base currency from you under the quote convention.
  • Ask: the price at which the market can sell the base currency to you under the quote convention.
  • Spread: the difference between the ask and bid.
Diagram — Bid, Ask and Spread

14Why Bid and Ask Matter

A beginner may look at one chart price and assume every transaction occurs at exactly that number. In reality, buy and sell execution is connected to the available bid and ask prices.

This is one reason a trader can see a position start with a small negative result immediately after opening. The spread separates the two sides of the market, and trading costs can therefore affect the position.

Do not ignore spread

A setup can look technically attractive on a chart but still have different execution characteristics when spreads widen.

15Currency Pair Price vs Pip Movement

The quoted price tells you the current relative valuation. Pip movement tells you how far that price has changed according to the instrument's quoting convention.

Concept Example What it tells you
Pair EUR/USD Which two currencies are being compared.
Price 1.1000 Current quoted relative value in the example.
Movement 1.1000 → 1.1050 The observed change in price.
Pip distance 50 pips The standardized movement distance under the relevant convention.

16What Makes One Currency Stronger?

A currency's relative value can be affected by many factors. These include interest-rate expectations, inflation expectations, economic growth, employment conditions, central-bank policy, government policy, capital flows, geopolitical developments and overall market risk sentiment.

These factors do not operate in isolation. Markets are forward-looking, meaning participants react not only to what has already happened but also to what they expect may happen next.

🏦 Central banks

Policy expectations can influence currency demand and valuation.

📊 Economic data

Inflation, employment and growth data can change expectations.

🌍 Risk sentiment

Global risk appetite can affect flows between currencies.

17Currency Pair Is a Relationship, Not a Single Story

This is one of the most important ideas in the entire lesson. If EUR/USD rises, it is tempting to say "the euro is going up." But the more precise interpretation is that EUR is gaining relative value against USD in that pair.

The euro could be strengthening against one currency while weakening against another at the same time. Likewise, the U.S. dollar can behave differently across different currency relationships.

Diagram — Relative Strength

18Currency Pair Inversion

If a pair is inverted, the relationship changes direction. For example, EUR/USD and USD/EUR describe reciprocal relationships, although the market convention and availability of a quoted instrument may differ.

Mathematical idea

If EUR/USD = 1.20, then the reciprocal relationship is approximately USD/EUR = 1 ÷ 1.20 = 0.8333.

This reciprocal concept becomes useful when understanding currency conversions and cross-rate relationships.

19Cross Rates

A cross rate describes the relationship between two currencies that can be derived through another currency. Historically, many cross relationships could be calculated through USD.

For example, if you know EUR/USD and GBP/USD, you can conceptually derive EUR/GBP from the relationship between those two rates.

20Understanding Pair Direction

Before looking for a buy or sell opportunity, first understand what the pair represents. Then determine what a rising or falling price means for the base and quote currencies.

📈 If the pair rises

The base currency is gaining relative value against the quote currency over the observed period.

📉 If the pair falls

The base currency is losing relative value against the quote currency over the observed period.

21How Currency Pairs Relate to Trading Sessions

Forex is a global market, and different currencies can become particularly active around the economic and financial centers associated with them. Session activity can influence liquidity, volatility and spreads.

🌏 Asian hours

Pairs involving JPY, AUD and NZD may receive significant attention from traders watching Asian-market developments.

🇬🇧 European hours

EUR and GBP pairs can become highly active as European markets open.

🇺🇸 U.S. hours

USD-related pairs can experience significant activity around U.S. economic releases and market participation.

Do not treat sessions as guarantees

A currency pair can move at any time. Session tendencies are context, not a promise of direction or volatility.

22Liquidity and Currency Pairs

Liquidity refers broadly to the ability to transact in a market with relatively efficient execution and without causing large price changes from individual orders.

Highly traded currency pairs generally have deeper participation than many less-traded exotic pairs. However, liquidity can change during the day and around major events.

💧 Higher liquidity

Often associated with tighter spreads and more continuous market participation, though conditions can vary.

🌊 Changing liquidity

News, market openings, holidays and unusual conditions can change available liquidity.

23Spread Differences Between Pairs

Not all currency pairs have the same spread. More heavily traded pairs often have tighter spreads during liquid conditions, while less-traded instruments can have wider spreads.

A trader comparing two setups should therefore consider not only the chart pattern but also the trading conditions of the specific instrument.

Factor Possible effect What a beginner should do
Market liquidity Can affect spread and execution quality. Check current conditions.
Major news Can cause rapid movement and spread changes. Know the economic calendar.
Trading session Participation may rise or fall. Understand when your chosen pair is active.
Pair type Major, cross and exotic pairs can behave differently. Do not assume all pairs trade identically.

24Currency Pair and Volatility

Volatility describes the degree and speed of price variation. Different currency pairs can display different volatility characteristics, and the same pair can behave differently under different market conditions.

Higher volatility can create larger price movement but also larger uncertainty. It should never be interpreted as "more volatility = easier profit."

Important

Movement creates opportunity and risk at the same time.

25Currency Pair and Correlation

Some currency pairs can show relationships because they share currencies or are influenced by common macroeconomic factors. These relationships are often called correlations.

Correlation is not fixed. It can strengthen, weaken or reverse as market conditions change. Therefore, correlation should be treated as context, not as a permanent trading rule.

🔗 Positive relationship

Two instruments may sometimes move in similar directions.

↔️ Inverse relationship

Two instruments may sometimes move in opposite directions.

⚠️ Changing relationship

Historical correlation does not guarantee future correlation.

26Do Not Double Your Risk Accidentally

Because multiple currency pairs can share the same currency, taking several positions can create overlapping exposure. For example, several trades involving USD can all be influenced by the same broad USD movement.

This means that counting each position separately may underestimate the total economic exposure of a trading plan.

Risk-awareness lesson

Before taking several positions, identify which currencies are shared across those positions. Multiple trades are not always independent.

27Currency Pair and Trading Costs

The visible chart is not the complete trading environment. Depending on the broker and account, costs can include spread, commissions and other applicable charges.

Costs matter particularly when a trader is targeting very small price movements. A strategy that expects a tiny movement must account for the relationship between expected movement and transaction costs.

↔️ Spread

Difference between bid and ask.

💳 Commission

May apply depending on the account structure.

⚠️ Slippage

Actual execution can differ from the expected price under some conditions.

28Reading a Pair Before Opening a Chart

A disciplined beginner can gather basic information before looking for a trade setup.

  1. Identify the currency pair.
  2. Identify the base currency.
  3. Identify the quote currency.
  4. Read the current quote.
  5. Check the instrument's decimal precision.
  6. Check the spread and normal trading conditions.
  7. Know the relevant market session.
  8. Check for major scheduled economic events.
  9. Only then move into chart structure and strategy analysis.

31Quote Precision

Different instruments can be displayed with different numbers of decimal places. A platform may also display fractional pip information.

Therefore, never determine the value of a price movement only by counting decimal places from memory. Confirm the instrument specification and the convention being used.

Beginner habit

Before calculating pips or monetary risk, check the pair's quote precision.

32Why Currency Pairs Matter for Price Action

Later in Strativo Academy you will study candles, swing highs and lows, higher highs, higher lows, lower highs, lower lows, support, resistance, liquidity and market structure.

All of those concepts are observed on the price of an instrument. Before interpreting the chart, you must know exactly what that price represents. A chart is not simply "going up or down"; it is showing a changing relative value between two currencies.

Connection to Price Action

Currency pair → price → candles → swings → structure → analysis.

33Currency Pair Selection

Beginners often make the mistake of selecting a pair only because it is moving quickly. A better approach is to understand the instrument first.

Good questions

  • Do I understand the currencies involved?
  • Do I understand the normal spread?
  • Do I understand when it is most active?
  • Do I understand the instrument's volatility?

Bad selection logic

  • "It moved a lot, so it must be easy."
  • "The candle is large, so I should enter."
  • "This pair always moves in one direction."
  • "A large target guarantees more profit."

34Common Beginner Mistakes With Currency Pairs

❌ Confusing base and quote

Always identify the left and right currencies before interpreting direction.

❌ Thinking a pair is one currency

A pair is a relationship between two currencies.

❌ Ignoring the quote price

The number tells you how the base is valued in the quote currency.

❌ Treating all pairs identically

Majors, crosses and exotics can have different trading characteristics.

❌ Ignoring spread

Trading costs can matter, especially for short-term strategies.

❌ Ignoring shared exposure

Several trades can involve the same currency and therefore overlap.

❌ Assuming correlation is permanent

Relationships between pairs can change with market conditions.

❌ Confusing movement with profit

Price movement alone does not determine monetary profit or loss.

38Final Summary

A Forex currency pair is a relationship between two currencies. The first currency is the base and the second is the quote. The displayed price tells you how much quote currency represents one unit of the base currency.

Understanding this relationship is more important than memorizing a list of symbols. Once you know how to read the pair, you can correctly interpret whether price movement represents an increase or decrease in the base currency's relative value.

You also need to understand that different pairs can have different liquidity, spreads, volatility and market behavior. A major pair, a cross and an exotic pair should not automatically be treated as identical.

One sentence to remember

A CURRENCY PAIR IS A RELATIVE-VALUE RELATIONSHIP: THE FIRST CURRENCY IS THE BASE, THE SECOND IS THE QUOTE, AND THE PRICE TELLS YOU HOW MUCH QUOTE CURRENCY REPRESENTS ONE UNIT OF BASE.
