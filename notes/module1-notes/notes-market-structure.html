01. What Is Market Structure?

Market structure is the way price forms a sequence of swing highs and swing lows over time. It helps describe whether price is generally moving upward, downward, or sideways.

01. WHAT IS MARKET STRUCTURE?VISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

Think of market structure as the roadmap created by price.

02. Swing Highs and Swing Lows

A swing high is a local turning point where price moves upward and then turns downward. A swing low is a local turning point where price moves downward and then turns upward. Not every tiny fluctuation should automatically be labelled as a major swing.

02. SWING HIGHS AND SWING LOWSVISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

Find the turning points first; label their relationships second.

03. The Four Core Structure Terms

Once swing highs and swing lows are identified, compare each new swing with a previous swing. This produces Higher High, Higher Low, Lower High and Lower Low.

03. THE FOUR CORE STRUCTURE TERMSVISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

HH and HL describe higher structure; LH and LL describe lower structure.

04. Higher High — HH

A Higher High occurs when price creates a new significant swing high above the previous significant swing high. Compare meaningful highs rather than tiny intrabar movements.

05. Higher Low — HL

A Higher Low occurs when price pulls back but the new significant swing low remains above the previous significant swing low. Repeated HLs can help describe upward structure.

06. Lower High — LH

08. Uptrend Structure

An uptrend is generally characterized by a sequence of Higher Highs and Higher Lows. Price can still have bearish candles and pullbacks inside an uptrend.

09. Downtrend Structure

A downtrend is generally characterized by a sequence of Lower Lows and Lower Highs. Price can still have bullish candles and temporary rallies inside a downtrend.

LH + LL + LH + LL generally describes bearish/downtrend structure.

10. Sideways or Range Structure

Not every market is trending. Sometimes price moves between relatively defined upper and lower areas without producing a clean directional sequence.

REMEMBER

11. How to Identify Structure Step by Step

Start with the chart, find obvious meaningful turning points, compare highs, compare lows, label HH/HL/LH/LL, and then study the sequence. Beginners often become confused by trying to label every tiny movement.

11. HOW TO IDENTIFY STRUCTURE STEP BY STEPVISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

Start broad → identify swings → compare → label → study the sequence.

12. Structure vs Individual Candles

A single candle describes one completed period. Market structure describes relationships between important turning points over multiple periods. This prevents a temporary candle movement from being mistaken for a structural change.

12. STRUCTURE VS INDIVIDUAL CANDLESVISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

Candle = local information. Structure = relationships across time.

13. Internal and Major Swings

Charts contain smaller swings inside larger swings. A lower-timeframe movement may be meaningful internally while still being a pullback inside a larger trend. Always define which swing scale you are analysing.

13. INTERNAL AND MAJOR SWINGSVISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

Structure has scale. Define the swing level before drawing conclusions.

14. Trend Pullbacks

An uptrend can move up, pull back, form an HL, and attempt another HH. A downtrend can rally into an LH before attempting another LL. Pullbacks are not automatically reversals.

14. TREND PULLBACKSVISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

Pullback ≠ reversal. Compare the new swing with prior structure.

15. Impulse and Correction

An impulse is a directional movement that travels strongly away from an area. A correction is a counter-move against recent directional movement. Structure analysis asks what the correction does to important swing points.

15. IMPULSE AND CORRECTIONVISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

Study how the correction interacts with previous swing points.

16. Structure Sequence in an Uptrend

A simple bullish sequence can look like: starting low → HH → HL → HH → HL → HH. The relationship between successive swing points is the important part.

17. Structure Sequence in a Downtrend

A simple bearish sequence can look like: starting high → LL → LH → LL → LH → LL. Short rallies can exist inside the sequence without automatically cancelling the broader structure.

18. Market Range Boundaries

In a range, price often reacts around an upper resistance area and a lower support area. The middle can contain less obvious directional structure. Treat boundaries as areas rather than perfect lines.

18. MARKET RANGE BOUNDARIESVISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

Upper area + lower area + repeated reactions = a useful range map.

19. Support and Structure

Support can become structurally important when price repeatedly reacts around a lower area and subsequent lows hold or advance. Support and structure are related but are not the same concept.

20. Resistance and Structure

Resistance can become structurally important when price repeatedly reacts around an upper area and subsequent highs fail or decline. Resistance and structure are related but are not identical.

28. BOS vs CHOCH

BOS commonly describes a break of a relevant structural point, often in the direction of existing structure. CHOCH commonly describes a potential change in the character of that structure. Different educators use the terms differently.

29. Structure Break vs Fake Break

Price can temporarily move beyond a structural point and then return. This false-break situation should not automatically be recorded as a lasting structural change. Study the close, return and following movement.

30. Breakout and Retest

After price breaks a structural level, it may return toward that area. This is often called a retest, but a retest is not guaranteed. Study break → possible return → reaction → continuation or failure.

34. Liquidity and Swing Highs/Lows

Swing highs and lows can attract attention because traders monitor those areas. Price may interact with a prior high or low before continuing or changing behaviour. First identify the swing; then study the reaction.

35. Equal Highs and Equal Lows

Sometimes price forms highs or lows around a similar level instead of producing a clearly higher or lower swing. Do not force HH or LH labels when the comparison is effectively equal.

35. EQUAL HIGHS AND EQUAL LOWSVISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

36. Major Swing vs Minor Noise

If every small fluctuation becomes a swing, the chart can contain conflicting labels. Use clear meaningful turning points and keep the chosen swing scale consistent.

37. Structure in Consolidation

During consolidation, price may alternate between small highs and lows without establishing a strong directional sequence. Do not force a trend label when structure is compressed and unclear.

37. STRUCTURE IN CONSOLIDATIONVISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

Compression can produce messy structure; do not force direction.

38. Structure Transition

A market can transition from trend to range, range to trend, or one directional structure to another. Observe changes in the sequence of important swings rather than declaring a transition from one candle.

38. STRUCTURE TRANSITIONVISUAL STUDY DIAGRAM
REMEMBER

Structure changes through sequences, not isolated candles.

39. Common Beginner Mistakes

Common errors include labelling every candle as a swing, mixing timeframes, calling every wick a BOS, treating every CHOCH as a reversal, ignoring equal highs/lows, and assuming trends continue forever.

40. Practical Structure Reading Process

45. Final Market Structure Framework

The complete framework is: identify timeframe, find meaningful swings, compare each new high and low with its reference point, label the relationship, read the sequence, identify the market state, and monitor structural breaks or transitions.

🎯 Final Market Structure Framework

1. TIMEFRAME

Choose the structural scale you are analysing.

2. SWINGS

Find meaningful swing highs and swing lows.

3. COMPARISON

Compare new highs with prior highs and new lows with prior lows.

4. LABEL

Use HH, HL, LH or LL when the comparison is clear.

5. SEQUENCE

Read several structural points together.

6. STRUCTURAL EVENT

Observe BOS, possible CHOCH, breakout, retest or failure.

CORE RULE

Market structure organizes what price has done so far. It does not guarantee what price will do next. Keep swing scale, timeframe and terminology consistent.
