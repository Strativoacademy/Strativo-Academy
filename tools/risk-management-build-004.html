FOUNDATION

What Is a Stop Loss?

🛡️

A Stop Loss is a predefined price level where a trader plans to exit a trade if the market moves against the trade idea.

A Stop Loss helps define where the trade idea is considered invalid and helps establish the amount that can be planned at risk.

KEY CONCEPT Stop Loss = Defined Exit Point

The Stop Loss should be connected to the trade setup, not chosen simply because a particular amount of money feels comfortable.

ENTRY $100

Planned entry price.

STOP LOSS $95

$5 price distance from entry.

RISK DISTANCE $5

The distance used when determining position size.

02
IMPORTANT CONCEPT

Stop Loss Distance Changes Position Size

If the planned monetary risk stays the same, a wider Stop Loss generally requires a smaller position size, while a narrower Stop Loss generally allows a larger position size.

NARROWER STOP Smaller Distance

A smaller price distance can require a larger position size to reach the same planned monetary risk.

WIDER STOP Larger Distance

A larger price distance can require a smaller position size to maintain the same planned monetary risk.

⚠️

Position size should not be increased simply because a Stop Loss is close. The calculation must account for the amount planned at risk and the Stop Loss distance.

SIMPLIFIED EDUCATIONAL FORMULA Position Size = Planned Risk Amount ÷ Stop Loss Distance

This simplified formula is used in this learning example. Real position sizing can also depend on instrument specifications, contract size, pip value, tick value, and account currency.
