01
FOUNDATION

What Is Risk-Reward Ratio?

⚖️

Risk-Reward Ratio compares the amount planned to be risked with the potential reward of a trade.

For example, a 1:2 ratio means the potential reward is two times the amount planned at risk.

BASIC FORMULA Risk-Reward Ratio = Risk : Potential Reward

If the planned risk is $10 and the potential reward is $20, the setup has a 1:2 risk-reward ratio.

IMPORTANT Risk-Reward Ratio does not guarantee a winning trade.

A ratio describes the relationship between planned risk and potential reward. It does not predict whether the trade will actually reach the target.

02
EXAMPLES

Understanding 1:1, 1:2 & 1:3

1 : 1 Risk $10 → Potential Reward $10

The potential reward equals the planned risk.

1 : 2 Risk $10 → Potential Reward $20

The potential reward is two times the planned risk.

1 : 3 Risk $10 → Potential Reward $30

The potential reward is three times the planned risk.

PLANNED RISK $20

Amount planned to be lost if the Stop Loss is reached.

POTENTIAL REWARD $40

Potential reward in a 1:2 example.

RATIO 1 : 2

$20 risk compared with $40 potential reward.

⚠️

A higher potential reward does not automatically make a trade better. The quality of the setup, market structure, entry, Stop Loss and target all matter.
