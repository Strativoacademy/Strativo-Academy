Drawdown & Loss Recovery

01
FOUNDATION

What Is Drawdown?

📉

Drawdown is the decline in account value from a previous peak to a lower point.

In simple terms, if an account reaches a peak and then falls because of losses, the decline from that peak is called drawdown.

BASIC FORMULA Drawdown % = (Peak − Current Value) ÷ Peak × 100

Example: An account rises to $1,000 and later falls to $900. The drawdown is $100, or 10% of the peak.

KEY IDEA A loss percentage and the required recovery percentage are not the same.

After an account falls, the remaining capital is smaller. Therefore, the percentage gain required to return to the original peak becomes larger.

02
RECOVERY

Why Loss Recovery Gets Harder

The larger the drawdown, the larger the percentage gain required to return to the previous account peak.

5% LOSS $1,000 → $950

The account needs approximately 5.26% growth from $950 to return to $1,000.

10% LOSS $1,000 → $900

The account needs approximately 11.11% growth from $900 to return to $1,000.

20% LOSS $1,000 → $800

The account needs 25% growth from $800 to return to $1,000.

ORIGINAL PEAK $1,000

The previous account high.

CURRENT VALUE $800

A 20% drawdown from the $1,000 peak.

RECOVERY NEEDED 25%

$800 must grow by 25% to return to $1,000.

⚠️

Drawdown management is about controlling losses before they become difficult to recover from. Avoid increasing risk simply because an account is down.
