FOUNDATION

What Is Risk Per Trade?

💰

Risk per trade is the amount of account capital a trader plans to put at risk on one trade.

It should be defined before entering the trade so that the trader knows the maximum planned loss if the Stop Loss is reached.

KEY CONCEPT Account Risk × Risk Percentage = Planned Risk Amount

For example, if an account contains $1,000 and the planned risk is 1%, the planned risk amount is $10.

ACCOUNT $1,000

Total account value used in the example.

RISK 1%

Planned percentage of the account.

PLANNED RISK $10

1% of $1,000 equals $10.

02
ACCOUNT RISK

Understanding Risk Percentage

A risk percentage converts an account balance into a planned monetary risk amount for a trade.

BASIC FORMULA Planned Risk = Account Balance × Risk % ÷ 100

The percentage determines how much of the account is planned to be exposed to loss on one trade.

0.5% RISK Lower Planned Exposure

On a $1,000 account, 0.5% represents $5 of planned risk.

1% RISK Example Risk Level

On a $1,000 account, 1% represents $10 of planned risk.

2% RISK Higher Planned Exposure

On a $1,000 account, 2% represents $20 of planned risk.

⚠️

A percentage-based risk rule does not guarantee a particular trading result. It simply defines a planned amount of account exposure before a trade is taken.
