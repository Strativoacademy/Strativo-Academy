FOUNDATION

What Is Position Sizing?

📐

Position sizing determines how large a trade can be while keeping the planned monetary risk within the trader's defined limit.

The position size should be calculated from the amount you are willing to risk and the distance to your Stop Loss.

BASIC EDUCATIONAL FORMULA Position Size = Planned Risk ÷ Stop-Loss Distance

In this simplified example, if the planned risk is $100 and the Stop-Loss distance is $10 per unit, the position size is 10 units.

KEY IDEA Wider Stop Loss → Smaller Position Size

If the planned monetary risk stays the same, increasing the distance to the Stop Loss generally requires reducing position size.

02
STOP-LOSS DISTANCE

Why Stop-Loss Distance Matters

When planned risk is fixed, the distance between the entry and Stop Loss affects how large the position can be.

SMALLER DISTANCE $100 Risk ÷ $5 Distance = 20 Units

A smaller distance allows a larger position in this simplified example.

MEDIUM DISTANCE $100 Risk ÷ $10 Distance = 10 Units

Doubling the distance reduces the calculated position size.

WIDER DISTANCE $100 Risk ÷ $20 Distance = 5 Units

A wider Stop-Loss distance requires a smaller position to maintain the same planned risk.

PLANNED RISK $100

The amount the example trader plans to risk.

STOP DISTANCE $10 / Unit

The simplified loss per unit if the Stop Loss is reached.

POSITION SIZE 10 Units

$100 ÷ $10 = 10 units.

⚠️

Real position sizing can depend on the instrument, contract specifications, pip or tick value, currency conversion, leverage and broker rules. The calculator in this lesson uses a simplified educational model.
