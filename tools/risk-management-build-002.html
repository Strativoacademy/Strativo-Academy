What Is Position Sizing?

📐

Position size connects your risk to your trade.

Position sizing is the process of deciding how large a trade should be based on the amount you are willing to risk and the distance to your Stop Loss.

SIMPLIFIED EDUCATIONAL FORMULA Position Size = Risk Amount ÷ (Stop Loss × Pip Value)

In this lab, pip value means the dollar value of one pip for one standard lot. Real broker instruments can have different contract specifications.

RISK AMOUNT How much money you are willing to lose.
STOP LOSS How many pips away your protective stop is.
PIP VALUE The dollar value of one pip for the selected lot basis.
02
WHY IT MATTERS

Why Position Size Matters

The same account risk can require very different position sizes when the Stop Loss distance changes.

SMALL STOP Larger position may be possible

A smaller pip distance can require more units to reach the same planned dollar risk.

LARGE STOP Smaller position may be required

A wider pip distance means each lot has more room to lose, so position size must be reduced.

⚠️

Position sizing should be calculated before entering the trade. Do not increase size simply because you want to recover a previous loss.
