FOUNDATION

What Is Risk of Ruin?

🛑

Risk of ruin describes the possibility of losing enough capital that continuing to trade becomes extremely difficult or impossible.

There is no single simple percentage that predicts whether a trader will be ruined. It depends on factors such as risk per trade, win rate, reward-to-risk ratio, number of trades and available capital.

IMPORTANT PRINCIPLE Higher risk per trade → larger account swings

Increasing the amount risked on every trade can make both winning and losing streaks have a much larger effect on account equity.

KEY IDEA Protecting capital comes before trying to maximize returns.

A trader who controls downside has more capital available to continue learning and trading after a losing period.

02
DRAWDOWN CONTROL

Consecutive Losses & Maximum Drawdown

A losing streak can reduce account equity quickly. The effect becomes larger as the percentage risk per trade increases.

1% RISK 10 Losses ≈ 9.56% Drawdown

When each loss removes 1% of the remaining account, ten consecutive losses leave approximately 90.44% of the starting balance.

2% RISK 10 Losses ≈ 18.29% Drawdown

Repeated 2% losses from the remaining balance create a much larger drawdown.

5% RISK 10 Losses ≈ 40.13% Drawdown

Large risk per trade can make a losing streak significantly more damaging.

LOW RISK Smaller Equity Swings

A losing streak has a comparatively smaller effect on the account.

HIGH RISK Larger Equity Swings

Each loss removes more capital and increases drawdown more quickly.

GOAL Control Maximum Drawdown

The objective is not to avoid every losing trade, but to prevent losses from becoming destructive.

⚠️

The examples above are mathematical illustrations, not predictions of future trading results. A real risk-of-ruin assessment requires more information than risk percentage alone.
