Lesson 6 - Order Types

Market orders execute a trade immediately at the best available current price. Pending orders, such as limits and stops, are instructions to automatically open or close a trade only when the price reaches a specific pre-defined level.

Order Types

Welcome to Lesson 6! In trading, knowing when to buy or sell is great, but knowing how to tell your broker to do it is just as important.

An Order is simply a message you send to your broker. It tells them exactly what you want to buy or sell, and at what price.

By learning the different types of orders, you will have full control over your money. You can even set up trades to happen automatically while you are asleep!

📘 Simple Definition

An Order is an instruction you give your broker to place a trade for you.

What is an Order?

Before you can enter a trade, you have to tell your broker what to do. This message is called an Order.

Think of it like ordering food at a restaurant. You tell the waiter (your broker) exactly what you want. The waiter then goes to the kitchen (the market) to get it for you.

Every single time you buy or sell in Forex, you are using an order.

📘 Simple Definition

Order = Your Instructions

It tells the broker how fast you want the trade, and at what price.

🔄 How Brokers Make It Happen

When you send an order to buy, your broker looks inside the market for someone who wants to sell.

When you want to sell, they look for someone who wants to buy. They match you up!

STEP 3 OF 10

Market Order

A Market Order is the simplest type of order. It means you want to Buy or Sell RIGHT NOW at whatever the current price is.

If you are in a rush and do not want to wait for a specific price, you use a Market Order.

⚡ Instant Action

  • Buy Now: You enter the trade instantly at the current buying price.
  • Sell Now: You enter the trade instantly at the current selling price.

💡 Best Time to Use It

Use a Market Order when getting into the trade quickly is more important than getting a perfect price.

For example: If big news comes out and the price starts shooting up, you might hit "Buy Now" to join the ride immediately.

⚠ Beginner Mistake

Because Market Orders happen instantly, the price might change a tiny bit in the split second it takes to click the button.

You might get a slightly different price than you expected.

STEP 4 OF 10

Pending Orders

What if you don't want to buy right now? What if you want to wait until the price gets cheaper?

That is where a Pending Order comes in. A Pending Order tells the broker: "Do not buy or sell right now. Wait until the price hits my special number, and THEN open the trade."

If the price never reaches your number, the trade never happens!

🗂️ The Four Types of Pending Orders

There are 4 main pending orders. They depend on what you expect the price to do:

  • 1. Buy Limit: You think the price will drop, hit the floor, and bounce back UP.
  • 2. Sell Limit: You think the price will rise, hit the ceiling, and fall back DOWN.
  • 3. Buy Stop: You think the price will rise, smash through the ceiling, and keep going UP.
  • 4. Sell Stop: You think the price will drop, smash through the floor, and keep falling DOWN.

🌍 Did You Know?

Pending orders let you set up your trade, turn off your computer, and go outside! The broker will open the trade for you automatically if the price hits your target.

STEP 5 OF 10

Buy Limit & Sell Limit

Let's talk about the first two pending orders: Limit Orders.

You use a "Limit" order when you expect the price to hit a wall and bounce back the other way.

📉 Buy Limit (Catching the dip)

You place this order below the current price.

Example: The price is falling. You think it will hit a cheap price and then bounce back up. You place a Buy Limit at the bottom so you can buy it at the cheapest price possible before it goes up.

📈 Sell Limit (Catching the top)

You place this order above the current price.

Example: The price is rising. You think it will hit a high ceiling and then fall back down. You place a Sell Limit at the top to start a sell trade right before it drops.

🧠 Memory Trick

Think of the word LIMIT as a BOUNCE.

You are waiting for the price to hit its limit and turn around!

STEP 6 OF 10

Buy Stop & Sell Stop

Now let's look at the other two pending orders: Stop Orders.

You use a "Stop" order when you think the price will smash through a wall and keep going in the same direction. Traders call this a "Breakout."

📈 Buy Stop (Jumping on the rocket)

You place this order above the current price.

Example: The price is going up. You think if it crosses a certain line, it will explode much higher. You place a Buy Stop above that line. When the price hits it, your Buy trade opens automatically to join the ride up.

📉 Sell Stop (Jumping on the slide)

You place this order below the current price.

Example: The price is going down. You think if it drops past a certain floor, it will crash even lower. You place a Sell Stop below that floor. When the price hits it, your Sell trade opens automatically to ride it down.

🧠 Memory Trick

Think of the word STOP as "DON'T STOP!"

You expect the price to hit your line and go straight through it without stopping.

STEP 7 OF 10

Stop Loss & Take Profit

This is the most important step in this lesson! Once your trade is open, you need a plan to close it.

You do this using two automatic tools: a Stop Loss (SL) and a Take Profit (TP).

🛡️ Stop Loss (SL) - Your Safety Seatbelt

A Stop Loss is an automatic order that closes your trade if you are losing money.

Why it matters: If you buy, but the market suddenly crashes, the Stop Loss will kick you out of the trade early. It takes a small loss so that your whole account isn't destroyed.

Golden Rule: NEVER open a trade without a Stop Loss!

🎯 Take Profit (TP) - Your Piggy Bank

A Take Profit is an automatic order that closes your trade when you reach your winning goal.

Why it matters: The market might hit your winning number and then quickly reverse. A Take Profit grabs your winnings instantly and puts the money safely in your account balance before the market can take it back.

STEP 8 OF 10

Real Trading Example

Let's see how all of this works together with a super simple example using the EUR/USD currency pair.

📊 Let's say the EUR/USD price is exactly 1.1000 right now.

  • Market Order (Buy Now): You click Buy. The trade opens instantly at 1.1000.

  • Buy Limit (Wait for a bounce): You want to buy it cheaper. You place a Buy Limit at 1.0950. The trade only starts if the price drops down to 1.0950.

  • Buy Stop (Wait for a breakout): You want to make sure it's really going up. You place a Buy Stop at 1.1050. The trade only starts if the price climbs up to 1.1050.

🛡️ Keeping the Trade Safe

Okay, let's say your trade is now open and you bought at 1.1000.

1. Set Stop Loss (SL): You put your SL at 1.0950. If the trade drops against you, it automatically closes so you don't lose any more money.

2. Set Take Profit (TP): You put your TP at 1.1100. If the trade goes up and hits this number, it closes automatically, and you get to keep the profit!

⭐ Remember

Not every trade needs a Market Order.

Professional traders often use Pending Orders because they allow them to plan their trades in advance instead of making emotional decisions.

The best traders don't chase the market—they wait patiently for the market to come to them.
